## When this applies
Summer outages with no shore power or not enough generator for the AC. A closed camper in Florida sun easily passes 100 °F inside.

## Do this
1. **Shade:** park or pitch a tarp or shade cloth over the camper's sunny side and roof; the awning out on the shady side (retract it before storms).
2. **Block windows from outside if you can,** or inside with reflective insulation (Reflectix) cut to fit, shiny side out, during the day.
3. **Vent:** open the roof vents and run vent fans exhausting out; open low windows on the shady side so cooler air comes in low and hot air leaves high.
4. **Fans on you:** 12 V clip fans on the power station move air across your skin, which is what cools you.
5. **Evaporative tricks:** a damp towel or shirt in front of a fan, a damp sheet at night, a spray bottle, feet in a bucket of water.
6. **Cook outside** and keep heat-making devices off inside.
7. **Night:** open everything up when it cools, close it before the sun hits in the morning.
8. **Daytime:** spend the hottest hours in the shade outside or somewhere with power.
9. **A thermometer** in the camper tells you when it's safer outside.

## Warning signs: get help
- Dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating: [Heat exhaustion](#/a/heat-exhaustion). Confusion: [Heat stroke](#/a/heat-stroke).
- The dog panting hard and not settling: cool him first ([Dog first aid](#/a/dog-first-aid)).

## Do NOT
- Do not run a generator close to the camper to power AC; keep it 20+ feet away.
- Do not leave the dog in a closed camper in summer.

## What you need
- Shade cloth or tarp, Reflectix, roof vent fans, 12 V fans, a power station, a thermometer.
