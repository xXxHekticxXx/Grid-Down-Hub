## When this applies
A tree limb or wind damages the camper or a shed roof and rain is coming.

## Do this
1. **Safety first:** wait for wind and lightning to pass; use a sturdy ladder footed by a second person. Camper roofs can be slick and some won't support your weight in the middle; stay over the frame.
2. **Inside:** catch drips with buckets, move valuables, start drying (mold starts in 1 to 2 days).
3. **Remove debris** from the damaged area.
4. **Size the tarp** to extend at least 3 to 4 feet past the damage on all sides, and over the ridge if possible, so water runs off rather than under.
5. **Roll the edges around 2×4 boards** and screw the boards down through the tarp, or on a camper, strap the tarp over and under with ratchet straps or cord.
6. **Smaller holes on a camper:** clean and dry, then cover with RV roof repair tape (EternaBond or similar) or butyl tape and a patch.
7. **Check after every rain** and retighten.

## Warning signs: get help
- Sagging roof, cracked frame, or electrical wiring wet: don't stay inside.

## Do NOT
- Do not climb onto a wet or windy roof or work alone.
- Do not nail through the middle of the tarp; every hole is a leak.

## What you need
- Heavy-duty tarps (several sizes), 2×4s, screws and a drill, ratchet straps, RV roof repair tape, butyl tape, a ladder.
