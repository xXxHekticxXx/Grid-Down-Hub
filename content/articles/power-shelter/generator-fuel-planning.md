## When this applies
Choosing, running and fueling a generator for a long outage.

## Do this
1. **Size it:** add running watts of what you'll run at once, plus the biggest starting surge. A camper fridge on electric (300 to 400 W), fans, lights and chargers fit easily under 2,000 W; the camper's rooftop AC usually needs a 3,000+ W generator (or a soft-start kit on a 2,200 W unit).
2. **Inverter generators** are quieter, use less fuel and make clean power for electronics.
3. **Fuel use:** a 2,000 W inverter at light load uses about 1 gallon every 4 to 8 hours. Check your model's spec and log real use in Tools → Fuel range.
4. **Run in shifts:** 2 to 3 hours morning and evening keeps a fridge cold and batteries charged and stretches fuel 3 to 4 times.
5. **Dual-fuel (gas and propane):** propane doesn't go stale and is stored safely in tanks, but gives a bit less power.
6. **Maintenance:** oil change after the first 20 hours, then per manual; spare oil, spark plug and air filter; run it monthly with stabilized fuel.
7. **Placement and safety:** see [Generator and CO safety](#/a/generator-co-safety).

## Warning signs: get help
- Surging, smoking or overheating: shut it down and let it cool.

## Do NOT
- Do not refuel a running or hot generator.
- Do not backfeed through a wall outlet.

## What you need
- Generator, oil, spark plug, fuel stabilizer, heavy-duty cords, a transfer switch or interlock if you'll power a building, a funnel.
- See [Storing fuel safely](#/a/fuel-storage).
