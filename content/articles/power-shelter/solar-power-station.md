## When this applies
Long outages and the field. Solar makes no noise, no fumes, needs no fuel, and works indoors (the battery part).

## Do this
1. **Add up daily watt-hours (Wh):** device watts × hours used. Examples: phone charge about 15 Wh; LED lantern 5 W × 5 h = 25 Wh; 12 V fan 20 W × 8 h = 160 Wh; laptop 60 W × 3 h = 180 Wh; small 12 V compressor fridge about 300 to 600 Wh a day in Florida heat; CPAP 30 to 60 W per hour of use.
2. **Size the battery** at about 1.5 times your daily need, so a cloudy day doesn't leave you empty.
3. **Size the panels:** in Central Florida, a panel produces roughly 4 to 5 times its rated watts in Wh per sunny day. 200 W of panels ≈ 800 to 1,000 Wh a day.
4. **Aim panels south,** tilted about 30°, out of shade; move them through the day if portable.
5. **Use it smart:** charge during the day while the sun refills it; run fans and fridges off DC (12 V) outputs, which waste less than the AC inverter.
6. **Keep the station cool and dry:** heat shortens battery life. Never leave it in the closed truck in summer sun.
7. **Store at about 50 to 80% charge** and top up every 3 months.

## Warning signs: get help
- A battery that swells, smells hot or hisses: move it outside away from anything flammable.

## Do NOT
- Do not run an air conditioner from a small station; a camper AC draws 1,300 to 1,800 W.
- Do not connect a power station to the camper's shore power inlet unless the camper's converter and charger setup is designed for it.
- Do not cover panels with wet leaves or let the dog chew the cables.

## What you need
- A LiFePO4 power station (1 to 2 kWh), folding or rigid panels with matching connectors, 12 V cables, a USB-C PD charger, a 12 V fan.
