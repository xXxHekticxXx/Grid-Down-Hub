## When this applies
Summer outages after storms, with heat indexes over 100 °F and no air conditioning.

## Do this
1. **Shift the day:** hard work before 10 a.m. and after 5 p.m.; rest in the shade or the coolest spot midday.
2. **Drink steadily** and check urine color: light yellow or clear means enough (CDC).
3. **Cool the body, not the room:** wet a shirt or towel and sit in moving air; soak feet in a bucket of water; cool showers; a battery fan with a wet cloth in front of it.
4. **Find cooling locations:** the county opens cooling centers and some shelters during long outages; libraries and stores with generators.
5. **Watch the dog:** shade, water, no midday walks, cool wet towels. See [Dog first aid](#/a/dog-first-aid).
6. **Medicines:** some make you more heat-sensitive; don't stop them, but be extra careful (CDC).
7. **Sleep:** see [Keeping the camper cool without AC](#/a/cool-camper-no-ac).

## Warning signs: get help
- Heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, cramps: [Heat exhaustion](#/a/heat-exhaustion).
- **Confusion or passing out: [Heat stroke](#/a/heat-stroke), call 911.**

## Do NOT
- Do not sit in a parked vehicle or closed camper without AC.
- Do not rely on alcohol or energy drinks for fluids.

## What you need
- Battery fans, a pop-up canopy, cooling towels, electrolyte powder, a thermometer to see how hot the camper really gets.
