## When this applies
Every time you run a generator. CPSC says one portable generator puts out as much carbon monoxide as hundreds of cars, and generators cause more than 100 deaths a year.

## Do this
1. **Outside only, more than 20 feet from the camper's doors, windows and vents,** exhaust pointing away and downwind (CDC).
2. **Battery CO alarm** inside the camper near where you sleep. Test it before storm season.
3. **Keep it dry:** under an open canopy or a generator tent, never in water.
4. **Connect with heavy-duty outdoor extension cords** rated for the load, or a transfer switch or interlock installed by an electrician.
5. **Let it cool before refueling;** fuel on a hot engine can ignite (Ready.gov).
6. **Chain or lock it** at night; generators are a top theft target after storms.
7. **Store fuel** in approved cans away from the camper and the generator.
8. **Run it in shifts** to save fuel: cool the fridge and charge batteries for a few hours, then shut it off.

## Warning signs: get help
- Headache, dizziness, nausea: get everyone outside and see [Carbon monoxide poisoning](#/a/carbon-monoxide-poisoning).

## Do NOT
- Do not run it inside the camper, a garage, shed, porch, or under the awning, even with doors open.
- **Do not plug it into a wall outlet ("backfeeding").** It can kill utility workers and neighbors and start fires.
- Do not overload it or use worn cords.

## What you need
- Battery CO alarm, heavy-duty cords, a canopy, a chain and lock, fuel stabilizer. Fuel planning: Tools → Fuel range.
