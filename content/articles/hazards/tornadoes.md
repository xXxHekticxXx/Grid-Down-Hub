## When this applies
Tornadoes come with hurricanes, tropical storms and spring squall lines in Central Florida, often fast and wrapped in rain with little visible warning. Signs: a loud roar like a freight train, a rotating cloud, flying debris.

## Do this
1. **Have a plan now:** which sturdy building you'll get to and how long it takes. On a tornado watch day, stay close to it.
2. **Tornado warning: go immediately** to a small, windowless interior room on the lowest floor of a sturdy building.
3. **Cover your head and neck** with your arms and anything padded (mattress, cushions, blankets). A helmet helps.
4. **In the truck:** if the tornado is far away and traffic is light, drive at right angles away from it. If caught in extreme wind or debris, park out of traffic, stay buckled, get your head below the windows and cover it (Ready.gov). Or, if you can safely get noticeably lower than the road (a ditch), lie there and cover your head.
5. **NOAA weather radio** with a tone alert wakes you at night; keep it programmed to Lake County (SAME 012069).

## Warning signs: get help
- After it passes: injuries, downed lines, gas smell. Call 911 and use the first aid pages.

## Do NOT
- Do not shelter in the camper, a shed or under a bridge or overpass.
- Do not wait to see the tornado.

## What you need
- NOAA weather radio, shoes and a flashlight by the bed, a plan for the dog.
