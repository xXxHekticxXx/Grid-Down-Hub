## When this applies
Before hurricane season and when a tropical storm or hurricane is forecast. A bumper-pull camper can roll or be pierced by debris in strong wind.

## Do this
**At the start of the season:**
1. **Trees:** remove dead trees and limbs that could fall on the camper, truck or driveway.
2. **Know where the camper would go:** a sheltered spot away from trees, or a storage lot, or hitch it up and take it with you if you evacuate early.
3. **Anchor kit:** if you'll leave the camper on site, auger-style ground anchors and straps rated for RVs on the frame.
4. **Paperwork:** photos of the camper, truck, tools and equipment for insurance, in My Plan.

**3 to 5 days out:**
1. **Bring in or tie down everything loose:** chairs, grill, propane tanks (upright, chained), trash cans, tools, plywood, lawn equipment.
2. **Retract the awning and slide-outs;** close vent covers; tape off roof vents if they leak.
3. **Shut the propane at the tank** before you leave.
4. **Disconnect shore power** and secure the cord.
5. **Move the truck to the highest open ground** away from trees, or take it with you.
6. **Gate and access:** a chainsaw in the truck so you can cut your way back in.
7. **Leave the camper for the storm;** go to your sturdy building or shelter.

## Warning signs: get help
- Tornado warning: get to the sturdy building immediately; see [Tornadoes](#/a/tornadoes).

## Do NOT
- Do not stay in the camper during a hurricane or tornado warning.
- Do not leave propane tanks loose; they become missiles and leak.

## What you need
- RV anchors and straps, ratchet straps, chains, a tarp, plastic sheeting, duct tape, the chainsaw.
