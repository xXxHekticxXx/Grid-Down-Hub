## When this applies
Florida's wildfire season peaks from about January to June, worst in a spring drought. The Ocala National Forest also uses prescribed (planned) burns. Grid-down conditions mean more debris burning and less firefighting.

## Do this
1. **Around the camper:** clear dead vegetation, pine needles and debris at least 30 feet out; keep grass short; store firewood and fuel away from the camper.
2. **Know two ways out** of the property and of any forest campsite.
3. **Evacuate immediately if told,** and leave early if fire is near and wind is pushing it toward you. Take the go bag and the dog.
4. **In smoke:** stay inside with windows closed, wear an N95 mask outside, run a small air purifier or a box fan with a furnace filter taped to it if you have power.
5. **Camping in the forest:** check fire restrictions (the Forest Service posts orders; in March 2026 the Ocala NF had Stage 1 restrictions limiting dispersed campers to propane). See [Camping in the Ocala National Forest](#/a/ocala-nf-camping).
6. **Trapped:** call 911 with your location, go to a cleared area, a road, a lake or a bare sandy spot; lie face-down and cover yourself.

## Warning signs: get help
- Burns or smoke inhalation: see [Burns](#/a/burns).
- Asthma flaring in smoke: [Asthma attack](#/a/asthma-attack).

## Do NOT
- Do not burn trash or debris on dry, windy days, or when burn bans are in effect.
- Do not drive into smoke you can't see through.

## What you need
- N95 masks, a garden hose and shovel, a rake, the go bag.
