## When this applies
Clearing fallen trees and limbs from the driveway, the camper and fences after a storm. Chainsaw injuries surge after hurricanes.

## Do this
1. **Gear (CDC):** hard hat, safety glasses, hearing protection, heavy gloves, chainsaw chaps from waist to boot, boots that cover the ankle.
2. **Saw ready:** sharp chain, correct tension, bar oil, working chain brake and chain catcher.
3. **Look first:** for power lines, nails, wire and fence in the wood, and for spring poles (bent limbs or trees under tension).
4. **Cut at waist level or below,** both hands on the saw, thumb wrapped around the handle, stand to the side of the cut path.
5. **Spring poles:** release tension with small shallow cuts on the inside of the bend, standing clear of where it will spring.
6. **Keep people 2 tree lengths (150 feet) from felling** and 30 feet from limbing (CDC).
7. **Clear cut wood as you go** so you have an escape path.
8. **Work in short sessions;** fatigue and heat cause accidents. Never cut alone if you can help it.

## Warning signs: get help
- Chainsaw cut: hard pressure; tourniquet if a limb is bleeding badly. See [Severe bleeding](#/a/severe-bleeding).

## Do NOT
- Do not cut above shoulder height or from a ladder.
- Do not cut with the tip of the bar (kickback).
- Do not cut trees touching power lines.

## What you need
- Chainsaw, chaps, helmet with face screen and ear muffs, gloves, wedges, a trauma kit within reach.
