## When this applies
The storm has passed and officials say it's safe to go out.

## Do this
1. **Listen for official information** (AlertLake, NOAA radio, local radio): road closures, boil water notices, open shelters and supply points.
2. **Return home in daylight.** Check the camper for structural damage, propane leaks (smell), and electrical damage before going in. If you smell gas, leave and shut the tank valve from outside.
3. **Photograph all damage** before cleaning up, for insurance.
4. **Generator outside only,** more than 20 feet from doors and windows; see [Generator and CO safety](#/a/generator-co-safety).
5. **Downed lines:** assume they're live; stay 35+ feet away and report them. See [Downed power lines](#/a/downed-power-lines).
6. **Clearing trees:** chaps, eye and ear protection, gloves; watch for spring poles; see [Chainsaw and storm cleanup](#/a/chainsaw-cleanup).
7. **Water and food:** follow boil notices; check food temperatures ([Fridge and freezer](#/a/fridge-freezer-food-safety)).
8. **Heat:** cleanup in a Florida summer with no AC is dangerous. Work early and late, drink, rest in shade.
9. **Mold:** dry out wet items within 24 to 48 hours or throw them out; see [Mold cleanup](#/a/mold-cleanup).
10. **Check on neighbors.**

## Warning signs: get help
- Injuries from cleanup: [Severe bleeding](#/a/severe-bleeding), [Cuts and infection](#/a/wounds-infection).
- CO symptoms (headache, dizziness, nausea) near a generator.

## Do NOT
- Do not wade in floodwater (sewage, chemicals, sharp debris, snakes, gators, live wires).
- Do not climb on the roof or into trees alone.
- Do not hire unlicensed contractors who ask for full payment up front.

## What you need
- Chainsaw with safety gear, tarps and screws or roofing nails, work gloves, boots, N95 masks, bleach, trash bags, the camera on your phone.
