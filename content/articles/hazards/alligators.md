## When this applies
Alligators live in all 67 Florida counties (FWC), in every lake around Sorrento and every spring and river in the Ocala NF. Most active from dusk to dawn and in warm months.

## Do this
1. **Keep a safe distance** (at least 30 feet; if it hisses or moves toward you, you're too close).
2. **Keep the dog on a leash and away from the water's edge.** Dogs look like prey to gators. This is the biggest risk for your dog.
3. **Swim only in designated areas in daylight,** without the dog.
4. **Fill water and clean fish** at spots with a clear view, not at dusk; dispose of scraps in trash.
5. **Run in a straight line** away if one charges on land; they rarely chase far.
6. **If grabbed:** fight back hard, hit or gouge the eyes and snout, make noise. Don't play dead.
7. **Nuisance alligator** (over 4 ft and a threat to people, pets or property): FWC Nuisance Alligator Hotline **866-FWC-GATOR (866-392-4286).**

## Warning signs: get help
- Any bite: control bleeding ([Severe bleeding](#/a/severe-bleeding)) and get medical care; gator bites get badly infected.

## Do NOT
- **Never feed alligators** (illegal and dangerous).
- Do not let the dog drink or swim at the edge of lakes and springs.
- Do not camp, sleep or clean game right at the waterline.

## What you need
- A leash. Common sense at the water's edge.
