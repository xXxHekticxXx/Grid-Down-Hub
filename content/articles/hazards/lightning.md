## When this applies
Any thunderstorm, most summer afternoons. If you can hear thunder, you're close enough to be struck; lightning can strike more than 10 miles from the rain.

## Do this
1. **Plan outdoor work** for the morning on storm days, and watch the sky to the south and west after noon.
2. **Thunder or a dark, building storm: go inside a substantial building or a hard-topped vehicle** with windows up. Don't wait for the rain.
3. **Stay inside 30 minutes after the last thunder.**
4. **Inside:** stay off corded phones and away from plumbing, plugged-in electronics, windows and doors (cell phones are fine).
5. **Caught outside with no shelter:** get off high ground and away from water, isolated trees, fences, power lines and metal. Keep moving toward shelter. There is no safe place outside.
6. **In camp:** the truck is your lightning shelter, not the tent or tarp.

## Warning signs: get help
- Someone struck: they're safe to touch. Call 911 and start CPR if needed; see [Lightning strike and electrical shock](#/a/lightning-electrical).

## Do NOT
- Do not shelter under a tree, a picnic pavilion, a carport or a tent.
- Do not swim, fish or boat in a storm.
- Do not lie flat on the ground.

## What you need
- A habit of checking the sky and radar before afternoon work. NOAA radio for warnings.
