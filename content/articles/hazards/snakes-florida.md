## When this applies
Any time outdoors, especially clearing brush, moving wood and debris, walking at night, near water and in palmetto scrub.

## Do this
Know the six (FWC):
1. **Eastern diamondback rattlesnake:** the largest venomous snake in North America. Heavy-bodied, diamond pattern outlined in cream, a rattle. Statewide; palmetto, pine flatwoods, scrub.
2. **Dusky pygmy rattlesnake:** small (under 2 ft), gray with dark blotches and a reddish stripe, a tiny rattle that sounds like an insect. Common around Lake County, in grass and near water.
3. **Timber (canebrake) rattlesnake:** north Florida; tan with dark crossbands and a rust stripe. Unlikely around Sorrento.
4. **Cottonmouth (water moccasin):** thick, dark, near water; may gape showing a white mouth. Many harmless water snakes look similar: treat any thick dark snake by water as a cottonmouth.
5. **Eastern (harlequin) coral snake:** slender, red, yellow and black bands, with red touching yellow, black snout. Shy; bites when handled.
6. **Copperhead:** Panhandle only.

Pit vipers (all but the coral snake) have a broad triangular head, cat-eye pupils and a pit between the eye and nostril.

**Avoid bites:** look before you step over logs or reach into woodpiles, palmetto, brush or debris; wear boots and long pants; use a flashlight at night; give snakes several feet of room; keep the dog leashed in brush.

## Warning signs: get help
- Any bite: [Snakebite](#/a/snakebite).

## Do NOT
- Do not try to kill or catch a snake; most bites happen that way.
- Do not handle a "dead" snake or a severed head.
- Do not rely on color rhymes alone; if unsure, leave it.

## What you need
- Snake boots or gaiters, leather gloves for debris, a flashlight.
