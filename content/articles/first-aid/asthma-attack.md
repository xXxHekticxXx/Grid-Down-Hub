## When this applies
Wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, short of breath, struggling to speak in full sentences, using neck and shoulder muscles to breathe. Smoke from wildfires or burning debris, mold after flooding, dust and cleaning chemicals can trigger it.

## Do this
1. **Sit them upright,** leaning slightly forward. Stay calm with them.
2. **Rescue inhaler (usually albuterol):** help them use it as their asthma action plan says. A spacer helps a lot.
3. **Get away from the trigger:** smoke, dust, chemicals, mold.
4. **No better within a few minutes, or getting worse:** repeat the inhaler as their plan says and **call 911.**

## Warning signs: get help
- Lips or fingernails blue or gray, can't talk, drowsy or confused, ribs sucking in with each breath: **call 911 now.**
- Rescue inhaler not working or needed more often than every 4 hours.

## Do NOT
- Do not have them lie flat.
- Do not use someone else's inhaler only when no other option exists; it is better than nothing in a severe attack.
- Do not clean mold or use bleach around a person with asthma; have someone else do it with windows open. See [Mold cleanup](#/a/mold-cleanup).

## What you need
- At least 30 days of asthma medicines and supplies (CDC), a spacer, a written action plan in My Plan.
- N95 masks for smoke.
