## When this applies
Florida still has hypothermia: soaked by rain or a lake in a cold front, a long night outside in wind with wet clothes, or an older person in an unheated camper. Early: shivering, clumsy hands, cold pale skin. Worse: stumbling, mumbling, confusion, drowsiness, then shivering stops.

## Do this
1. **Get them out of the cold, wind and rain:** the truck, the camper, a tarp shelter.
2. **Remove soaked clothing** in a sheltered spot and replace with dry layers. Damp (not soaked) fleece can stay on if you can't change it.
3. **Insulate from the ground** (a sleeping pad, cardboard, branches) and **cover the head and neck.**
4. **Wrap them:** dry insulation (sleeping bag, blankets), then a plastic or foil layer outside it to block wind and moisture.
5. **Warm the torso:** hot water bottles or large heat packs, wrapped in cloth, on the chest, armpits and back. Check the skin often for burns.
6. **Alert and can swallow:** warm (not hot) sweet drinks and high-calorie food.
7. Not breathing normally: [CPR](#/a/cpr).

## Warning signs: get help
- **Confused, mumbling, very sleepy, can't help take off their own wet clothes, very pale or blue: call 911** and keep rewarming.

## Do NOT
- Do not rub or massage arms and legs, or heat the arms and legs first.
- Do not put a confused or drowsy person in a warm shower or bath.
- Do not rely on little hand-warmer packets to rewarm someone.
- Do not give alcohol.

## What you need
- Emergency (foil) blankets, a sleeping bag, wool or fleece layers, rain gear, a vacuum bottle for hot drinks, large chemical heat packs.
