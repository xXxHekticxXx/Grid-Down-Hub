## When this applies
The power is out, pharmacies are closed or roads are blocked, and someone depends on daily medicines, some of which need refrigeration (insulin, some eye drops, some liquid antibiotics) or heat-sensitive storage (epinephrine pens, inhalers).

## Do this
1. **Keep at least a 30-day supply** of every daily medicine when your insurance allows early refills; many plans allow emergency refills when a state of emergency is declared.
2. **Write down every medicine, dose, prescribing doctor and pharmacy** in My Plan (Medical) and print it. Keep a photo of each label.
3. **Insulin (FDA):** unopened or opened vials and pens can be kept unrefrigerated between **59 °F and 86 °F for up to 28 days** and still work. Keep it as cool as you can, out of sunlight, never frozen. In a cooler, keep it off the ice (wrap it). In an emergency, insulin stored hotter may still be needed; use it and check blood sugar more often, and replace it as soon as you can.
4. **Insulin pump sets:** follow your pump's instructions; insulin in the pump that got hotter than 98.6 °F should be replaced.
5. **Other refrigerated medicines:** if the power has been out more than a day, discard those that say "refrigerate" unless the label or pharmacist says otherwise, and get a new supply (Ready.gov).
6. **Hot camper or truck:** store medicines in the coolest, shadiest spot, in an insulated bag; never on the dashboard.

## Warning signs: get help
- Running out of a medicine that can't be stopped suddenly (seizure, heart, blood pressure, psychiatric, steroid, blood thinner medicines): get a refill or care before you run out. Shelters and emergency clinics can help.
- Blood sugar running high or low despite your usual insulin.

## Do NOT
- Do not stop or change medicines on your own because of heat or shortage; call a pharmacist or doctor if you can.
- Do not freeze insulin or use insulin that has frozen.

## What you need
- An insulated medicine bag and a small cooler; frozen water bottles rotate well with a generator or solar fridge.
- The medicine list (My Plan → Medicines).
