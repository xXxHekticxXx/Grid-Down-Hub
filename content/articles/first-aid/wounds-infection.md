## When this applies
Everyday cuts, scrapes and punctures from tools, brush, nails, storm debris or fishing hooks. After a hurricane, injuries and infections from floodwater and debris are common, and help may be slow.

## Do this
1. **Wash your hands;** gloves if you have them.
2. **Stop bleeding** with firm pressure.
3. **Rinse the wound with lots of clean running tap water or bottled water** until you see no dirt or debris. Use a squeeze bottle or a zip bag with a pinhole for pressure. Water works as well as antiseptics.
4. **Wash around the wound** with soap and water.
5. **Cover clean wounds** with an occlusive dressing: petroleum jelly or antibiotic ointment under a bandage, or a film dressing. Waterproof bandages if you must be near floodwater.
6. **Leave dirty wounds, bites and punctures open** (covered loosely) rather than taping them tightly shut; closing them traps bacteria.
7. **Look at the wound every 24 hours** and re-dress it.
8. **Tetanus:** if your last tetanus shot was more than 5 years ago and the wound is dirty or a puncture, get a booster as soon as you can.

## Warning signs: get help
- **Spreading redness, swelling, warmth, pus, red streaks, increasing pain or fever: infection.** It needs a doctor and antibiotics.
- Fever with confusion, fast heart rate, fast breathing, clammy skin or extreme pain: this may be [sepsis](#/a/sepsis), an emergency.
- Deep or gaping cuts, cuts on the face or hand joints, anything embedded (wood, metal, soil), animal or human bites, punctures from dirty objects.

## Do NOT
- Do not pour hydrogen peroxide or alcohol into the wound; they damage tissue.
- Do not glue or tape shut a dirty wound or a bite.
- Do not wade in floodwater with an open wound.

## What you need
- Bandages of all sizes, gauze, tape, petroleum jelly or antibiotic ointment, a 60 mL irrigation syringe or squeeze bottle, gloves, tweezers, butterfly closures.
