## When this applies
Someone overheated who is now **confused, acting strange, slurring, staggering, having a seizure or passing out.** Skin may be hot and red, dry or still sweating. Body temperature 103 °F or higher. Heat stroke kills and damages organs fast; the faster you cool, the better they do.

Central Florida from May to October, a camper with no AC, hard work clearing storm debris, and no power to run fans: this is a real risk every summer.

## Do this
1. **Call 911.**
2. **Get them out of the heat:** shade, AC, a car with AC running.
3. **Strip excess clothing.**
4. **Cool them hard, right now. Best: put them in cold or cool water up to the neck** (a tub, a cattle trough, a kiddie pool, a lake edge with someone holding them) **for 15 minutes or until they're thinking clearly again,** whichever comes first. Hold their head above water.
5. **No tub?** Soak them with cold water from a hose or buckets and fan hard; cover them with ice-water-soaked towels or sheets and keep swapping them; ice packs to the neck, armpits and groin. Combine methods.
6. **Small sips of cool water only if they're awake and can swallow** normally.
7. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, start [CPR](#/a/cpr).

## Warning signs: get help
- Heat stroke always needs a hospital, even after cooling works.
- Seizure, vomiting, not waking up, dark urine or no urine.

## Do NOT
- Do not wait to "see if it passes."
- Do not give fluids to someone who is confused or can't swallow well.
- Do not give aspirin or acetaminophen to lower the temperature; they don't work for heat stroke.
- Do not leave them alone in water.

## What you need
- A tub, trough or kiddie pool you can fill, a hose or buckets, towels or sheets, a cooler of ice.
- A thermometer.
- See [Heat exhaustion and dehydration](#/a/heat-exhaustion) to catch it earlier, and [Keeping the camper cool without AC](#/a/cool-camper-no-ac).
