## When this applies
Life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that is not controlled by hard direct pressure, or that you can't hold pressure on (more than one injured person, you must move, or the limb is mangled or amputated). Modern tourniquets are safe: limb loss from a properly used tourniquet is rare, and they save lives.

## Do this
1. **Place it 2 to 3 inches above the wound,** between the wound and the heart, on the arm or thigh. Not on a joint. If you can't see the wound clearly or are in a hurry, go **"high and tight"**: as high on the limb as it will go.
2. **Pull the strap tight** so you can barely get your fingertips under it, then fasten it.
3. **Twist the windlass** (the rod) until the bleeding stops. This hurts. That is expected. Keep twisting until bright red bleeding stops completely and, ideally, there is no pulse at the wrist or foot beyond it.
4. **Lock the windlass** in its clip and secure the strap.
5. **Write the time** on the tourniquet or the person's forehead ("TQ 14:32"). Start the timer below: it counts up so you can tell the hospital how long it has been on.
6. **Still bleeding?** Tighten more. Still bleeding after that? Put a second tourniquet right above the first.
7. **Keep it on and get them to a hospital.** Recheck often that it hasn't loosened and that bleeding hasn't started again, especially after moving them.

::timer 120 Tourniquet on: tell the ER the time

**Improvised tourniquet** (only if you have no real one): a strip of cloth at least 2 inches wide (a folded triangle bandage or a torn shirt), wrapped twice, tied, a strong stick tied in as a windlass and twisted, then the stick tied down. Narrow cords, zip ties and belts don't work well and cause more damage. A commercial tourniquet is better; buy several.

## Warning signs: get help
- Everyone with a tourniquet needs a hospital, as fast as possible.
- If medical help is truly hours or days away (grid down, roads closed), a trained person may later convert the tourniquet to packing and a pressure bandage. ATP 4-02.11 in the Library describes it. Untrained: leave it on.
- Bleeding that restarts, a limb that turns blue below a loose tourniquet, or a person getting confused or very pale: move faster.

## Do NOT
- Do not loosen it to "let blood flow" or check the wound.
- Do not put it over a knee or elbow, or on the neck, chest or groin.
- Do not cover it with blankets or clothing where nobody will see it.
- Do not use a tourniquet for snakebite. It makes snakebite worse.
- Do not buy cheap knock-offs; their windlasses snap. Look for CAT (Combat Application Tourniquet) or SOF-T Wide from a reputable seller.

## What you need
- A commercial windlass tourniquet in every kit, the truck and on your belt or range bag. Practice putting it on your own arm and leg one-handed.
- Marker for the time.
- This phone: the tourniquet timer.
