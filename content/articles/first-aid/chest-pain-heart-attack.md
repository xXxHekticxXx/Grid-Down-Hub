## When this applies
Heart attack signs: pressure, squeezing, fullness or pain in the center or left chest lasting more than a few minutes or coming and going; pain in one or both arms, the jaw, neck or back; short of breath; cold sweat; light-headed; nausea; unusual tiredness. Women, older people and people with diabetes more often have the "other" symptoms without strong chest pain.

## Do this
1. **Call 911 right away.** Don't drive yourself.
2. **Have them stop and sit or rest** in a comfortable position.
3. **Aspirin:** if they are an adult, awake, not allergic to aspirin, and have not been told by a doctor not to take it, have them **chew one regular 325 mg aspirin or two to four 81 mg low-dose tablets** (162 to 325 mg total). Chewed works faster than swallowed.
4. **If they have their own nitroglycerin**, help them take it as prescribed.
5. **Loosen tight clothing. Stay with them** and watch breathing.
6. If they collapse and aren't breathing normally: **[CPR](#/a/cpr)** and an AED.

## Warning signs: get help
- Any of the signs above: call 911 first, think later.
- If phones are down: drive to the nearest ER (AdventHealth Waterman, Tavares) with someone else driving; don't wait at home.

## Do NOT
- Do not give aspirin to someone allergic to it, with active bleeding, or who is not fully alert.
- Do not let them "walk it off" or keep working.
- Do not dismiss it as heartburn without being checked.

## What you need
- Aspirin (81 mg chewable) in every first aid kit.
- A list of each person's heart medicines in My Plan.
