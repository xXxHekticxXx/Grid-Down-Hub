## When this applies
Life-threatening bleeding looks like: blood spurting or flowing fast, blood pooling on the ground, clothing or bandages soaking through, bleeding that does not slow with firm pressure, or a bleeding person who turns pale, dizzy, confused or sleepy. Someone can bleed to death in a few minutes, long before any ambulance arrives.

## Do this
1. **Protect yourself:** gloves if they are within reach; a plastic bag over your hand works. Don't delay for them.
2. **Call 911** or have someone call. Tell them "severe bleeding."
3. **Find where it is coming from.** Open or cut away clothing. Wipe away pooled blood so you can see the actual hole.
4. **Press hard, directly on the wound,** with gauze, a clean cloth or your bare hand. Use both hands, lock your arms and lean your body weight onto it.
5. **Keep pressing without peeking** for at least several minutes. If blood soaks through, put more cloth on top and keep pressing; don't lift the first layer.
6. **Arm or leg still bleeding badly with hard pressure? Put on a tourniquet now.** See [Tourniquet](#/a/tourniquet).
7. **Deep wound in the armpit, groin, neck or a big hole in a thigh or buttock** where a tourniquet won't go: pack it. See [Wound packing](#/a/wound-packing).
8. **Hemostatic gauze** (QuikClot, Celox and similar) helps when you press it into the wound; it still needs hard pressure on top.
9. **Once bleeding stops, wrap a pressure bandage** snugly over the dressing to hold it (an Israeli bandage, elastic wrap or strips of cloth). Check fingers or toes beyond it stay pink and warm.
10. **Keep them warm and lying down.** Blood loss makes people cold quickly, and cold blood doesn't clot. Treat for [shock](#/a/shock).

## Warning signs: get help
- Any bleeding that needed a tourniquet, packing or more than a few minutes of pressure needs a surgeon. Get to an ER: AdventHealth Waterman in Tavares is the nearest.
- Pale, sweaty, fast breathing, confusion or sleepiness after blood loss means shock: it is an emergency even if the bleeding has stopped.
- Bleeding from the chest or belly can't be stopped by pressure from outside. Get them to a hospital any way you can.

## Do NOT
- Do not lift the dressing to look; you will tear out the clot.
- Do not rely on "pressure points" or elevating the limb instead of direct pressure. They are not proven to work.
- Do not remove an object stuck in the wound (stick, knife, glass). Pad around it and press around it.
- Do not give food or drink if they may need surgery; small sips of water only if help is many hours away and they are awake.

## What you need
- Gloves, 2 or more rolls of gauze (hemostatic if you can get it), a pressure bandage, a commercial tourniquet (CAT or SOF-T Wide) for each vehicle, the camper and your belt kit.
- Trauma shears to cut away clothing.
- A permanent marker to write the tourniquet time.
