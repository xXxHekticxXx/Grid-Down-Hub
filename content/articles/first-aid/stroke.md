## When this applies
Symptoms that start **suddenly**: numbness or weakness of the face, arm or leg (often one side); confusion, trouble speaking or understanding; trouble seeing; trouble walking, dizziness or loss of balance; sudden severe headache with no known cause.

## Do this
1. **BE FAST check:**
   - **B**alance: suddenly off-balance or dizzy?
   - **E**yes: sudden trouble seeing?
   - **F**ace: ask them to smile. Does one side droop?
   - **A**rms: raise both arms. Does one drift down?
   - **S**peech: repeat a simple sentence. Slurred or strange?
   - **T**ime: any one sign, **call 911 now.**
2. **Write down the time symptoms started** (or when they were last seen normal). Doctors need it to choose treatment; the best treatments only work within hours.
3. **Keep them lying with the head slightly raised,** or on their side if vomiting.
4. **Stay with them;** if they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, [CPR](#/a/cpr).

## Warning signs: get help
- Symptoms that go away after a few minutes (a "mini-stroke") still need the ER today; a full stroke can follow.
- If 911 can't come: get them to an ER with someone else driving, not the person having symptoms.

## Do NOT
- Do not give aspirin for a possible stroke; some strokes are bleeding.
- Do not give food, drink or pills; swallowing may not work.
- Do not let them sleep it off.

## What you need
- The time. That's the most important thing you can bring.
