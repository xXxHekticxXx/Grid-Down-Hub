## When this applies
After a sting, bite, food or medicine: trouble breathing or wheezing, throat tightness, hoarse voice, swelling of the lips, tongue or face, widespread hives with dizziness or fainting, vomiting, or a feeling of doom. Can start within minutes. Fire ant swarms and wasp nests uncovered while clearing brush are common triggers here.

## Do this
1. **Call 911.**
2. **Epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen, Auvi-Q, generic) right away** if they have one: outer thigh, through clothing if needed, hold as the device instructs. Help them use it if they can't.
3. **Lay them flat** with legs raised, unless they're struggling to breathe; then let them sit up. If vomiting, lay them on their side.
4. **No improvement in 5 to 10 minutes and help isn't there yet: give a second dose** if one is available.
5. **Remove the stinger** if you see one, by scraping or plucking, quickly.
6. If they stop breathing normally, start [CPR](#/a/cpr).

::timer 5 Epinephrine given: second dose if no better

## Warning signs: get help
- Everyone who needed epinephrine must go to a hospital. Symptoms can come back hours later.
- Eye stings need a doctor even without anaphylaxis.

## Do NOT
- Do not wait to see if it gets worse before giving epinephrine. Giving it early is the treatment.
- Do not rely on an antihistamine (Benadryl) for breathing trouble; it only helps itching and hives.
- Do not have the person stand up or walk.

## What you need
- If anyone in the group has had a serious reaction: two auto-injectors with them at all times, kept out of the hot truck (heat ruins them).
- An oral antihistamine for mild itching and hives.
- See [Bee, wasp and fire ant stings](#/a/stings-fire-ants).
