## When this applies
Bleach, pool chlorine (calcium hypochlorite), drain cleaner, battery acid, fertilizer, pesticide, fuel or other chemicals splashed on the skin or into the eyes.

## Do this
1. **Protect yourself:** gloves, and don't spread it to other people or to the other eye.
2. **Dry powder: brush it off first,** then flush (some powders react with a little water).
3. **Flush the skin with running water for at least 15 minutes.** Take off contaminated clothing, jewelry and watches while flushing.
4. **Eye: flush with lots of clean tap water for 15 minutes,** holding the eyelids open, with the injured eye down so water runs away from the good eye. Saline or eyewash is fine if it's right there.
5. **Call Poison Help (1-800-222-1222)** with the container label: some chemicals need special care.

::timer 15 Keep flushing with water

## Warning signs: get help
- Breathing trouble, a large area exposed, or body-wide symptoms: call 911.
- Any chemical in the eye that still hurts or blurs vision after flushing: emergency care.
- Hydrofluoric acid (rust removers, some wheel cleaners): emergency even if it doesn't hurt yet.

## Do NOT
- Do not try to "neutralize" an acid with a base or the reverse.
- Do not stop flushing early because it feels better.
- Do not mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners: it makes toxic gas.

## What you need
- Water, lots of it. A gallon jug kept by the battery bank and the chemical shelf.
