## When this applies
After serious bleeding, burns, a crush injury, a severe infection or allergic reaction, a person can go into shock: not enough blood reaching the organs. Signs: pale or gray skin, cold and sweaty, fast weak pulse, fast breathing, thirst, anxiety, then confusion and drowsiness.

## Do this
1. **Call 911.**
2. **Stop any bleeding** first: [severe bleeding](#/a/severe-bleeding).
3. **Lay them flat** on their back. If they are vomiting or drowsy, roll them onto their side.
4. **Keep them warm,** even in summer: something under them (off the ground), a blanket or emergency blanket over them. Trauma patients lose heat fast and cold makes bleeding worse.
5. **Keep them still and calm;** tell them help is coming.
6. **Check breathing and alertness every few minutes.** Note the time things change.
7. If they stop breathing normally, [CPR](#/a/cpr).

## Warning signs: get help
- Shock is always an emergency.
- Getting sleepier, harder to wake, breathing changes, or pulse getting faster.

## Do NOT
- Do not give food or drink.
- Do not let them get up and walk around.
- Do not raise the legs if there is a leg, hip, back or head injury.

## What you need
- Emergency (foil) blankets, a sleeping pad or tarp to put under them.
