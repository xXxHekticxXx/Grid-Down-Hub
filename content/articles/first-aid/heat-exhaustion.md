## When this applies
In heat or after hard work: heavy sweating, cold clammy skin, fast weak pulse, nausea or vomiting, muscle cramps, tiredness, dizziness, headache, fainting. Dark urine or not peeing much means you're behind on fluids.

## Do this
1. **Stop working. Move to shade or AC.**
2. **Loosen clothing; put cool wet cloths on the skin** or sit in cool water; fan.
3. **Drink.** A sports drink or electrolyte mix (4 to 9% sugar) rehydrates best; water is fine. Sip steadily.
4. **Cramps:** stop, stretch gently, drink a sports drink, and don't go back to work until they're gone.
5. **Rest the remainder of the day** and keep drinking until urine is pale yellow.

**Prevent it:** work in the early morning and evening, drink before you're thirsty, take breaks in shade every hour, wear a wide-brimmed hat and light loose clothing, and watch the dog too.

## Warning signs: get help
- **Confusion, slurred speech, staggering or passing out: this is [heat stroke](#/a/heat-stroke). Call 911 and cool them fast.**
- Vomiting so they can't keep fluids down, symptoms getting worse, or lasting over an hour.
- Cramps lasting over an hour, or anyone on a low-salt diet or with heart problems.

## Do NOT
- Do not keep working "to finish the job."
- Do not drink alcohol or lots of caffeine in the heat.
- Do not give fluids to someone who is confused or can't swallow.

## What you need
- Water you can reach: at least 1 gallon a person a day, more when working in heat. See [Water storage](#/a/water-storage).
- Electrolyte powder or tablets, or the home recipe in [Diarrhea and oral rehydration](#/a/diarrhea-ors).
- Shade: a pop-up canopy or tarp. Hat. Cooling towel.
