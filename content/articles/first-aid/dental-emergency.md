## When this applies
A permanent (adult) tooth knocked out by a fall or blow; also broken teeth and toothache when dentists are closed.

## Do this
**Knocked-out adult tooth:**
1. **Pick it up by the crown (the white part), not the root.**
2. **Rinse it briefly (under 10 seconds)** if it's dirty, without scrubbing.
3. **Push it gently back into the socket** facing the right way, and have them bite on gauze or a cloth to hold it.
4. **Can't put it back?** Store it in (best first): oral rehydration solution or wrapped in plastic cling wrap; then cow's milk or the person's own saliva (in a cup, or in their cheek if they're an adult and won't swallow it); then egg white.
5. **See a dentist right away.** Minutes matter.

**Toothache or broken tooth:** rinse with warm salt water, ibuprofen or acetaminophen as labeled, cold pack on the cheek, soft food on the other side.

## Warning signs: get help
- Facial swelling, fever, swelling under the jaw or trouble swallowing or breathing with a toothache: dental infection spreading, urgent care.
- Baby teeth are not replanted; just see a dentist.

## Do NOT
- Do not store a tooth in tap water.
- Do not scrub the root or let it dry out.
- Do not put aspirin on the gum.

## What you need
- Gauze, a small container, a temporary dental filling kit (pharmacy) for lost fillings.
