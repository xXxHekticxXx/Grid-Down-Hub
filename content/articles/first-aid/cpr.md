## When this applies
Someone does not respond when you tap them and shout, and is not breathing or is only gasping. Gasping is not breathing. Do not spend time feeling for a pulse; untrained people can't find one reliably, and CPR on someone who turns out to have a pulse rarely hurts them.

This includes drowning, lightning strike, electrocution and suspected overdose once the person stops breathing normally.

## Do this
1. **Make the scene safe.** Downed lines, traffic, water, fire, smoke or CO: get the person out first if you can do it without becoming a second victim.
2. **Shout for help and call 911** on speaker, or send someone. If you have no signal, start CPR and keep trying 911 and text-to-911 between cycles with someone else's hands if there is a second person.
3. **Adult:** heel of one hand on the lower half of the breastbone, center of the chest; other hand on top. Lock your elbows, shoulders straight over your hands.
4. **Push at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep, no more than 2.4 inches, 100 to 120 times a minute.** Let the chest come all the way back up between pushes. Start the metronome above; push on every beat.
5. **If trained and willing: 30 pushes, then 2 breaths.** Tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose, seal your mouth over theirs, blow for 1 second until the chest rises, let it fall, give the second. Then straight back to pushing. If you are not trained, or not willing, keep pushing without breaths.
6. **AED:** the moment one arrives, turn it on and do exactly what it says. Keep pushing while the pads go on.
7. **Do not stop** except to let the AED analyze, to give breaths, or to switch rescuers every 2 minutes (pauses under 10 seconds). Stop when help takes over, or the person moves, opens their eyes or breathes normally. Then roll them on their side and watch their breathing.

**Child (1 year to puberty):** one or two hands, push about 2 inches (a third of the chest depth). Alone with no phone: give about 2 minutes of CPR before leaving to call. 30 pushes to 2 breaths alone; two trained rescuers 15 to 2.

**Infant (under 1 year):** heel of one hand, or both thumbs with your hands wrapped around the chest, just below the nipple line. Push about 1½ inches. The old two-finger method is no longer recommended (AHA 2025). Cover the mouth and nose with your mouth for gentle puffs.

**Choking that turned into collapse:** start CPR beginning with pushes. Before each set of breaths, look in the mouth and take out anything you can see. No blind finger sweeps. See [Choking: adult and child](#/a/choking-adult-child).

**Suspected opioid overdose:** CPR with breaths, plus naloxone if you have it. See [Opioid overdose](#/a/opioid-overdose).

## Warning signs: get help
- Anyone who needed CPR needs a hospital, even if they wake up.
- After they start breathing, they can stop again. Stay with them, on their side, and keep checking.
- If no help can come (grid down, no ambulance), keep going as long as you physically can, switching with others every 2 minutes. If there is no response after a long effort with no AED and no help on the way, stopping is a decision only you can make at the scene.

## Do NOT
- Do not wait to "check for a pulse."
- Do not push on the belly or the ribs at the side; stay on the breastbone.
- Do not lean on the chest between pushes; let it come all the way up.
- Do not stop for long to give breaths; two breaths should take under 10 seconds.
- Do not put a person in water or a shower to "revive" them.

## What you need
- Your hands. Nothing else is required.
- A CPR face shield or pocket mask (in the first aid kit) if you will give breaths.
- An AED if one exists nearby (churches, schools, gyms, sheriff's cars).
- This phone: the metronome above keeps the rate at 110 a minute and counts to 30.
