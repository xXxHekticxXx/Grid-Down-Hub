## When this applies
Sudden shaking or jerking, stiffening, falling, staring and not responding, or confusion that comes and goes. Most seizures stop on their own within a few minutes. Seizures can come from epilepsy, head injury, heat stroke, low blood sugar, alcohol withdrawal, poisoning or a high fever in small children.

## Do this
1. **Stay calm and stay with them. Start timing.**
2. **Ease them to the ground** if they are falling.
3. **Move hard and sharp things away.**
4. **Turn them gently onto one side** with the mouth pointing down so saliva can drain.
5. **Put something soft and flat under the head** (a folded jacket). Take off glasses. Loosen anything around the neck.
6. **Check for a medical bracelet or necklace.**
7. **When the shaking stops,** keep them on their side, talk calmly, and let them rest. They'll be confused and tired for a while.

::timer 5 Seizure: call 911 if still going

## Warning signs: get help
Call 911 if:
- The seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, or another one starts soon after.
- They have trouble breathing or don't wake up afterward.
- They are hurt, the seizure happened in water, they are pregnant, or they have diabetes.
- It's their first seizure ever.

## Do NOT
- Do not put anything in their mouth. People don't swallow their tongues.
- Do not hold them down or try to stop the movements.
- Do not give food, water or pills until they are fully awake.

- A timer (this phone).
- If someone in the group has epilepsy: their rescue medicine and written seizure plan in My Plan.
