## When this applies
Anyone shot, including a negligent discharge at camp or a ricochet. The bullet path is unpredictable: a hole in the hip may involve the belly; a hole in the shoulder may involve the chest. Rifle wounds (your AR-15) are much more destructive than pistol or .22 wounds.

## Do this
1. **Make it safe first.** Stop the threat or get away from it. Unload and secure every firearm involved before treating.
2. **Call 911.** Say "gunshot wound," where on the body, and your location (the app shows your coordinates in three formats: tap Map).
3. **Massive bleeding first:** arm or leg → [tourniquet](#/a/tourniquet) high and tight. Groin, armpit, neck → [pack the wound](#/a/wound-packing). Everywhere else → hard direct pressure.
4. **Find every hole.** Cut away clothing and check front, back, armpits, groin and buttocks with a gloved hand. Run your hand along the back and look at the glove for blood. Exit wounds are often larger.
5. **Chest or upper back wound:** see [Open chest wound](#/a/chest-wound). Leave it open or put a vented chest seal on; watch breathing.
6. **Belly wound:** cover with a moist, clean dressing. Do not push anything back in. Do not give food or drink.
7. **Head wound:** gentle pressure only, keep them on their side if they're vomiting or drowsy.
8. **Keep them warm and lying down;** treat for [shock](#/a/shock). Don't routinely immobilize the spine for a gunshot wound; it slows care and costs lives.
9. **Get them to a trauma surgeon** by the fastest means. Orlando Health ORMC in downtown Orlando is the area's Level I trauma center; the nearest ER (Waterman in Tavares) can stabilize and transfer.

## Warning signs: get help
- Every gunshot wound needs a hospital, even small .22 wounds; bullets travel and fragment inside.
- Trouble breathing, a chest that doesn't rise evenly, blue lips, a hard swollen belly, confusion, or a racing pulse: the injury inside is worse than it looks.

## Do NOT
- Do not probe for the bullet.
- Do not wash out deep wounds; cover them.
- Do not seal a chest wound airtight on all four sides with plastic and tape unless you will watch closely and peel a side up if breathing gets worse.
- Do not handle any firearm except to make it safe.

## What you need
- Trauma kit in the truck and in your range bag: tourniquet, hemostatic gauze, pressure bandage, vented chest seals (two: entry and exit), trauma shears, gloves, emergency blanket, marker.
- Firearm safety rules every time; see [Firearms in the field](#/a/firearms-in-the-field).
