## When this applies
Something is stuck in the throat. **Mild:** they can cough hard, talk or make noise. **Severe:** weak or no cough, can't speak, can't breathe, grabbing the throat, lips turning blue.

For a baby under 1 year, see [Choking: infant](#/a/choking-infant).

## Do this
**Mild choking:** let them keep coughing. Stay with them and watch for it to become severe.

**Severe choking (AHA 2025):**
1. **Have someone call 911.**
2. **5 back blows.** Stand slightly behind them, support their chest with one hand and lean them forward. Give 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your other hand.
3. **5 abdominal thrusts.** Stand behind them, wrap your arms around their waist. Make a fist, thumb side in, just above the belly button and below the ribs. Grab it with your other hand and pull sharply in and up, 5 times.
4. **Keep alternating 5 back blows and 5 thrusts** until the object comes out or they become unresponsive.
5. **Pregnant, or too big to reach around:** give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts (hands on the lower half of the breastbone, pull straight back).
6. **They go limp:** lower them to the ground, call 911 if nobody has, and **start [CPR](#/a/cpr)** with chest pushes. Each time you open the mouth for breaths, look for the object and take it out only if you can see it.

**Choking and alone:** call 911 (even if you can't talk, they can trace the call), then give yourself abdominal thrusts with your fist, or thrust your upper belly hard against the back of a chair or a counter edge.

## Warning signs: get help
- Anyone who needed abdominal thrusts should be checked by a doctor: thrusts can injure organs inside.
- Coughing, wheezing or trouble swallowing that continues after the object is out.

## Do NOT
- Do not slap the back of someone who is still coughing well; let the cough work.
- Do not do blind finger sweeps in the mouth; you can push the object deeper.
- Do not give water to "wash it down."

## What you need
- Nothing but your hands.
