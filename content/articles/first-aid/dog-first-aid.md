## When this applies
Your 90-lb dog is hurt, overheated, bitten or poisoned. An injured, scared dog may bite even the person he loves; protect yourself first.

## Do this
1. **Muzzle if he's in pain** and not vomiting or struggling to breathe: a soft muzzle or a strip of gauze looped over the snout and tied behind the ears.
2. **Bleeding:** press a clean cloth firmly over the wound and **hold for at least 3 minutes before checking.** If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top; don't remove the first layer. Severe bleeding: straight to the vet.
3. **Heatstroke** (heavy panting, drooling, weakness, confusion, vomiting, dark or sticky gums): move him to shade or AC, **drape towels soaked in room-temperature water on his neck, armpits and groin, rewet often, and put a fan on him.** Then to the vet right away. Offer small amounts of water once he's cooling.
4. **Snakebite:** keep him as still as possible, carry him if you can, and go to an emergency vet. Assume it's venomous. Take a photo of the snake only if it's safe.
5. **Broken leg:** lay him on a flat surface; use a board, blanket or tarp as a stretcher for a big dog. Don't attempt a splint unless you're sure.
6. **Shock** (pale gums, weak pulse, shallow breathing): keep him warm, quiet and lying down; head level with the body; vet immediately.
7. **Poisoning (bufo/cane toad in the mouth, chemicals, antifreeze, sago palm seeds, medicines):** wipe and rinse the gums from the side with a wet cloth for toads; then call ASPCA Poison Control **888-426-4435** or Pet Poison Helpline **855-764-7661** (fees may apply) and head to a vet.

## Warning signs: get help
- Collapse, pale or blue gums, trouble breathing, a swollen hard belly with retching (bloat in a big dog: minutes matter), seizures, heavy bleeding, snakebite, heatstroke.

## Do NOT
- Do not put him in a tub of cold or ice water for heatstroke; it can make it worse.
- Do not give human pain medicines (ibuprofen, naproxen, acetaminophen); they poison dogs.
- Do not induce vomiting unless a vet or poison line tells you to.

## What you need
- Dog first aid kit: gauze, vet wrap, a soft muzzle, saline, tweezers and a tick tool, a towel, a digital thermometer (normal is about 101.5 to 102 °F), his records and vet numbers in My Plan → Dog.
- Water: about 1 ounce per pound a day, so about 90 oz (¾ gallon) for him, more in heat and work. See [The dog on the move](#/a/dog-on-the-move).
