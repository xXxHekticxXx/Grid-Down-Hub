## When this applies
Burns from fire, a camp stove, grease, hot water, a hot engine or exhaust, or the sun. Superficial: red, painful, no blisters. Partial thickness: blisters, very painful. Full thickness: white, leathery, brown or black, may not hurt.

## Do this
1. **Stop the burning:** stop, drop and roll; get away from the heat; take off smoldering clothing unless it's stuck.
2. **Cool it right away with clean, cool running water for 5 to 20 minutes.** If you don't have running water, pour clean water steadily. For small burns with unbroken skin, ice wrapped in cloth for up to 10 minutes is a fallback.
3. **Take off rings, watches, belts and tight clothing** near the burn before it swells.
4. **Small blistered burn (smaller than the palm) at home:** after cooling, a thin layer of petroleum jelly, antibiotic ointment, honey or aloe vera gel, then a clean non-stick dressing. Change daily.
5. **Cover larger burns loosely** with a clean dry cloth or non-stick dressing while you get help.
6. **Pain:** acetaminophen or ibuprofen.

## Warning signs: get help
- **Smoke inhalation: soot around the nose or mouth, singed nose hairs, hoarse voice, coughing or trouble breathing: call 911.** The airway can swell shut.
- Any full-thickness burn; blistered burns larger than the person's palm; burns on the face, hands, feet, genitals or across a joint; burns that go all the way around a limb; electrical or chemical burns.
- Signs of infection later: spreading redness, pus, fever.

## Do NOT
- Do not put butter, oil, toothpaste or mustard on burns.
- Do not pop blisters.
- Do not cool a large burn on a small child for so long that they get cold; watch for shivering.
- Do not pull off clothing stuck to the burn.

## What you need
- Clean water, non-stick dressings (Telfa), gauze rolls, petroleum jelly or burn gel, aloe vera gel.
