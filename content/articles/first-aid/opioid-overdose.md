## When this applies
Someone is very sleepy or won't wake up, breathing is slow, noisy or stopped, lips may be blue, pupils may be tiny. It can come from street drugs (often fentanyl-laced), someone's pain pills, or a child finding medicine.

## Do this
1. **Shout and rub hard on the breastbone.** No response and not breathing normally: **call 911** and **start [CPR](#/a/cpr) with breaths.**
2. **Give naloxone:** nasal spray into one nostril, press the plunger fully. It is safe even if it turns out not to be an opioid.
3. **Keep doing CPR.** Naloxone takes a few minutes to work and breathing support matters most during that time.
4. **No response in 2 to 3 minutes:** give another dose in the other nostril if you have one.
5. **They start breathing:** roll them on their side and stay with them. They may wake up confused, sick or upset.

## Warning signs: get help
- Everyone given naloxone needs medical care. Naloxone wears off before many opioids do and they can stop breathing again.
- Seizure, or not waking even after naloxone.

## Do NOT
- Do not leave them alone to "sleep it off."
- Do not put them in a cold shower or ice, or try to make them vomit.
- Do not give a second dose before CPR is under way.

## What you need
- Naloxone nasal spray is sold over the counter at pharmacies. Keep one in the first aid kit and the truck.
