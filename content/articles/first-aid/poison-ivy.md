## When this applies
Poison ivy grows everywhere in Central Florida: vines on trees and fences, ground cover in woods, "leaves of three." Poison sumac grows in swamps. Clearing brush after a storm is the most common exposure. The oil (urushiol) also stays on tools, gloves, clothes and the dog's fur.

## Do this
1. **Wash exposed skin with soap and water (or dish soap) as soon as you can.** Right away removes nearly all of the oil; after 10 minutes about half; after 30 minutes much less. Still worth doing hours later.
2. **Wash clothes, gloves and tools,** and bathe the dog with gloves on.
3. **Itch:** cool compresses and oatmeal baths. Over-the-counter hydrocortisone and antihistamines help some people but evidence is weak.
4. **Keep fingernails short;** don't scratch open the blisters.

## Warning signs: get help
- Rash on the face, eyes, mouth or genitals, or covering large areas.
- Trouble breathing after breathing smoke from burning poison ivy: emergency.
- Signs of infection: pus, spreading redness, fever.

## Do NOT
- **Do not burn poison ivy.** The smoke can cause severe lung reactions.
- Do not touch your face after handling brush until you've washed.

## What you need
- Dish soap, long sleeves, gloves, a change of clothes; learn to recognize it in [Deadly and dangerous plants](#/a/toxic-plants-florida).
