## When this applies
Obvious deformity, swelling or bruising, severe pain with movement or can't move or bear weight, or bone through the skin (open fracture). When in doubt between a sprain and a break, treat it as a break.

## Do this
1. **Bleeding from an open fracture:** treat bleeding first ([Severe bleeding](#/a/severe-bleeding), tourniquet if needed). Cover the wound with a clean dressing.
2. **Splint it the way you found it.** Don't straighten a bent limb unless you must to transport them safely and you can't otherwise.
3. **Padding first, then something stiff:** a SAM splint, a board, a rolled magazine, sticks, a folded sleeping pad. It should reach past the joints above and below the break.
4. **Tie it on** with cloth strips, tape or an elastic wrap, snug but not tight. Leave fingers or toes showing.
5. **Check beyond the splint** every 15 minutes: color, warmth, feeling. Loosen ties if fingers or toes go pale, blue, cold or numb.
6. **Arm:** splint, then a sling and a swathe to hold it against the body. **Ankle:** a pillow or sleeping pad wrapped around it works well.
7. **Cold pack** (wrapped in cloth) 20 minutes at a time; raise it if comfortable; pain reliever.

## Warning signs: get help
- **Limb beyond the injury is blue, purple or pale: call 911 now.**
- Thigh bone (femur), pelvis or hip fractures: these can bleed heavily inside. Call 911; treat for [shock](#/a/shock).
- Any open fracture, broken bone in the neck or back, or a joint that looks dislocated.

## Do NOT
- Do not push a bone back under the skin.
- Do not test it by having them walk on it.
- Do not tie a splint so tight that it cuts off circulation.

## What you need
- A SAM splint (folds flat, weighs 4 oz), elastic wraps, triangular bandages for slings, tape.
