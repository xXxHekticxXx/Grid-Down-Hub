## When this applies
Any bite from a snake that is venomous or might be. Florida's venomous snakes: eastern diamondback, timber (canebrake) and dusky pygmy rattlesnakes, cottonmouth, copperhead (Panhandle only) and the eastern (harlequin) coral snake. Pit viper bites (all but coral snake) cause swelling, pain and bruising; coral snake bites may hurt little but can cause paralysis hours later. See [Florida's venomous snakes](#/a/snakes-florida).

## Do this
1. **Move away** from the snake. Don't try to catch or kill it; a photo from a safe distance is enough.
2. **Call 911.** Antivenom is the only real treatment and it's only at hospitals.
3. **Keep the person as still and calm as you can.** Walking speeds venom. Carry them or drive them if that doesn't delay care.
4. **Take off rings, watches, bracelets and tight clothing** from the bitten limb before it swells.
5. **Keep the limb in a comfortable position,** about level with the heart.
6. **Mark the edge of the swelling** with a pen and write the time every 15 to 30 minutes; doctors use this.
7. **Get to a hospital,** even if it doesn't hurt much (coral snake bites can look minor).
8. **Dog bitten:** same steps, keep them still, carry if you can, go to a vet fast.

## Warning signs: get help
- All venomous or possibly venomous bites need a hospital.
- Spreading swelling, bleeding gums, blood in urine, vomiting, faintness, trouble breathing or swallowing, drooping eyelids or slurred speech.

## Do NOT
- **Do not use a tourniquet**, constriction band or tight wrap.
- **Do not cut the bite or use suction** (including "venom extractor" kits).
- **Do not put ice on it** or soak it in ice water.
- Do not use electric shock, alcohol, or pain pills like aspirin or ibuprofen.
- Do not pick up a dead snake; reflex bites happen.

## What you need
- A pen, a phone for the photo and the call, a way to get to the ER fast.
- Snake boots or gaiters and a flashlight at night; watch where you step and reach.
