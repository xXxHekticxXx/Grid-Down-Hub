## When this applies
A stab, gunshot or impalement wound in the chest, upper back, or just below the collarbone or ribs. Air may hiss or bubble from it ("sucking chest wound").

## Do this
1. **Call 911 now.** This person needs a hospital.
2. **Control bleeding** around the wound with pressure next to it, not by stuffing it.
3. **Leave the wound open to air, or cover it with a loose clean dressing (gauze, a clean T-shirt), or put on a vented chest seal** if you have one. All three are acceptable first aid (AHA/Red Cross 2024).
4. **Look for an exit wound** on the back and treat it the same way.
5. **Let them sit up or lean toward the injured side** if that helps them breathe, unless other injuries prevent it.
6. **Watch breathing constantly.** If breathing gets worse after you cover the wound, **loosen or remove the dressing** to let air escape.

## Warning signs: get help
- Getting more short of breath, very fast breathing, blue lips, a swollen neck with bulging veins, or getting sleepy or confused: pressure is building inside the chest. Lift the dressing off the wound and move faster to a hospital.
- Coughing up blood.

## Do NOT
- Do not seal the wound airtight with plastic and tape on all four sides and then stop watching it.
- Do not pack a chest wound.
- Do not remove an object stuck in the chest; pad around it.
- Do not give anything to eat or drink.

## What you need
- Two vented chest seals (HyFin Vent, SAM Chest Seal) in the trauma kit.
- Gauze, gloves, trauma shears.
