## When this applies
Any bite or scratch from a mammal: a stray or unknown dog, a cat, raccoon, fox, bat, bobcat, otter, or a wild animal acting strangely. In Florida, raccoons, foxes, bats and cats carry rabies most often. Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms start, and completely preventable with shots given in time.

## Do this
1. **Wash the wound immediately with soap and water for 15 minutes.** This flushes out virus and bacteria. Then rinse well.
2. **Control bleeding** with pressure.
3. **Cover loosely**; don't tape a bite tightly closed.
4. **See a doctor or health department as soon as you can,** even for small bites. Tell them what animal, whether it was acting strangely, and whether it's someone's pet with up-to-date rabies shots.
5. **A bat in the room where someone was asleep** counts as a possible exposure even without a visible bite.
6. **Don't kill or handle the animal** if you can help it; note where it is so animal control can test or watch it.

## Warning signs: get help
- Deep bites, bites to the hand, face or a joint, cat bites (they get infected often): see a doctor today.
- Redness, swelling, pus or fever in the next days.
- Bites from bats, raccoons, foxes, skunks or any wild carnivore: rabies shots shouldn't wait.

## Do NOT
- Do not wait to see if the animal "seems sick."
- Do not close a bite with glue or tape.
- Do not approach sick, injured or unusually tame wildlife, especially in daytime.

## What you need
- Soap and plenty of clean water; gauze.
- Keep the dog's rabies vaccination current and the certificate in My Plan.
