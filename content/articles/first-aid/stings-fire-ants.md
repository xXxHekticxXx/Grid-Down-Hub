## When this applies
Honeybee, wasp, hornet and yellow jacket stings, and fire ant swarms, common in Florida yards, pastures and around downed trees. Normal reaction: pain, redness, swelling at the sting, itching; fire ants leave white pus bumps the next day.

## Do this
1. **Move away** from the nest or mound; brush fire ants off quickly (they cling).
2. **Honeybee stinger left in the skin: get it out right away,** by scraping with a card or plucking. Speed matters more than method.
3. **Wash with soap and water.**
4. **Cold pack** for pain and swelling.
5. **Itching:** an oral antihistamine or hydrocortisone cream.
6. **Pain:** acetaminophen or ibuprofen.
7. **Fire ant pustules:** leave them unbroken; keep clean.

## Warning signs: get help
- **Trouble breathing, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, hives all over, dizziness or vomiting: this is [anaphylaxis](#/a/anaphylaxis). Epinephrine and 911.**
- A sting in the eye itself.
- Many stings at once (dozens), or stings in the mouth or throat.
- Signs of infection after a few days.

## Do NOT
- Do not pop fire ant pustules.
- Do not swat at a wasp nest or mow over a mound; come back with bait or spray.

## What you need
- Antihistamine tablets, hydrocortisone cream, cold packs; an epinephrine auto-injector for anyone with a known severe allergy.
