## When this applies
Severe bleeding from a deep wound you can't put a tourniquet on: groin, armpit, side of the neck, buttock, or a large cavity in the thigh above where a tourniquet would fit. Also when a tourniquet on the limb didn't stop the bleeding.

Not for wounds in the chest or belly. Those can't be packed: cover them and get to a hospital.

## Do this
1. Call 911. Gloves on if you have them.
2. **Find the source.** Spread the wound open with your fingers if needed and wipe out pooled blood to find where it's coming from.
3. **Push gauze into the wound** with your fingers, starting right on the bleeding spot at the bottom. Hemostatic gauze is best; plain gauze or a clean cloth (T-shirt strip) still works.
4. **Keep feeding it in** and packing it tight until the wound is completely full and packing sticks out above the skin.
5. **Press hard with both hands, straight down on the packed wound, for at least 3 minutes** (10 minutes for plain gauze), with your full body weight.
6. **Wrap a pressure bandage** tightly over it to keep the pressure on. On the neck, wrap over the opposite armpit, not around the neck.
7. **Still bleeding through?** Don't pull the packing out. Push more in on top and press again.
8. Keep them warm and flat; treat for [shock](#/a/shock).

## Warning signs: get help
- Anyone you had to pack needs a surgeon. Get them to the ER even if it looks controlled.
- Growing swelling at the wound, or the person becomes pale, fast-breathing, confused: bleeding is continuing inside.

## Do NOT
- Do not pack the chest or the belly.
- Do not pull out packing to check it.
- Do not stop pressing early because your arms are tired: swap with someone, one hand replacing the other.

## What you need
- Hemostatic gauze (z-fold is easiest to pack), extra plain gauze rolls, a pressure bandage, gloves.
- Practice once on a wound-packing trainer or a cut piece of meat: it takes more gauze and more force than people expect.
