## When this applies
A person with diabetes (especially on insulin or sulfonylurea pills) who is shaky, sweaty, pale, hungry, irritable, confused, acting drunk, or has a blood sugar under 70 mg/dL. Skipped meals, extra work clearing storm damage, and heat all make it more likely.

## Do this
1. **If they are awake and can swallow safely:** give **15 to 20 grams of fast sugar.** Glucose tablets are best (4 to 5 g each, so 4 tablets). Otherwise: ½ cup (4 oz) juice or regular soda, or 1 tablespoon of sugar or honey, or a glucose gel tube.
2. **Wait 15 minutes, then recheck** how they are acting or their meter.
3. **Still low or still symptoms: give another 15 grams.**
4. **Once better, have them eat** a snack with carbs and protein (crackers and peanut butter, half a sandwich) if the next meal is more than an hour away.

::timer 15 Recheck blood sugar

## Warning signs: get help
- **Can't swallow safely, having a seizure or unresponsive: call 911.** If they have glucagon (nasal or injection) and you know how, use it. Roll them on their side.
- No improvement after two rounds of sugar.

## Do NOT
- Do not put food or drink in the mouth of someone who can't swallow.
- Do not use chocolate or peanut butter to treat the low; fat slows the sugar.
- Do not let them drive until fully recovered.

## What you need
- Glucose tablets in every kit and the truck.
- For anyone on insulin: see [Medicines without power](#/a/medications-no-power).
