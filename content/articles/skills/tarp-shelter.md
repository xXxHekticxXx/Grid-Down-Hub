## When this applies
Camping or bugging out, or a quick shelter when the camper is damaged. In Florida the main threats are rain, sun, insects and lightning, not cold.

## Do this
1. **Site:** flat, slightly raised ground that won't puddle; **no dead trees or limbs overhead** ("widowmakers"); away from ant mounds, game trails and the waterline (gators, flooding); 100 feet from springs and lakes per Forest Service rules.
2. **Ridgeline:** tie a cord tight between two trees about waist to chest height with a bowline on one end and a trucker's hitch on the other ([Knots](#/a/knots)).
3. **A-frame:** drape the tarp over the ridgeline, stake out the corners low and tight. Best for heavy rain.
4. **Lean-to:** tie one long edge to the ridgeline, stake the other to the ground, open side away from the wind; a fire in front reflects heat in on cold nights.
5. **Ground:** a ground sheet or sleeping pad under you, smaller than the tarp so it doesn't collect rain.
6. **Bugs:** a mosquito net hung inside is essential most of the year.
7. **Drip lines:** a short piece of string tied to the ridgeline just inside the tarp edge leads water off the cord before it runs into your shelter.

## Warning signs: get help
- Lightning: a tarp is no protection; get in the truck ([Lightning](#/a/lightning)).

## Do NOT
- Do not pitch in a dry wash or low spot.
- Do not cut live trees or limbs in the national forest for shelter.

## What you need
- An 8×10 or 10×12 tarp, 50 feet of paracord, 6 to 8 stakes, a mosquito net, a sleeping pad.
