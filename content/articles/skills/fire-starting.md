## When this applies
Cooking, boiling water, warmth on a cold front night, signaling, and keeping bugs off. Florida's humidity makes everything damp; preparation matters more than technique.

## Do this
1. **Check the rules:** fire restrictions in the Ocala NF (Stage 1 restrictions in 2026 limited dispersed campers to propane); burn bans at home in drought. See [Camping in the Ocala National Forest](#/a/ocala-nf-camping).
2. **Site:** an existing fire ring or a cleared spot of bare sand, away from overhanging branches, palmetto and grass; a bucket of water or sand ready.
3. **Gather everything first,** about twice what you think:
   - **Tinder:** dry grass, cattail fluff, palm fiber (from cabbage palm boots), Spanish moss (dried, check for chiggers), birch-like paper bark, cotton balls with petroleum jelly, dryer lint, and **fatwood** (resin-soaked pine from old stumps, Florida's best fire starter: shave it).
   - **Kindling:** dead twigs from pencil-lead to pencil thickness, snapped from standing dead branches (they're drier than ground wood).
   - **Fuel:** wrist-thick and larger dead wood, split to expose dry insides with the hatchet.
4. **Build:** a tinder bundle, a teepee or log-cabin of kindling over it with gaps for air.
5. **Light from the upwind side** with a storm match or lighter; with a ferro rod, scrape sparks straight into the tinder.
6. **Feed gradually,** small to large; blow gently at the base.
7. **Wet weather:** split wood to the dry core, make feather sticks (thin curls shaved along a stick but left attached), and build on a platform of sticks off wet ground.
8. **Put it out:** drown it, stir, drown again until cold to the touch (Forest Service).

## Warning signs: get help
- A fire escaping into grass or palmetto: call 911 and move upwind or to bare ground.
- Burns: [Burns](#/a/burns).

## Do NOT
- Do not use gasoline to start or speed up a fire.
- Do not leave a fire unattended or build one on a windy dry day.
- Do not burn poison ivy or oleander.

## What you need
- Storm matches, two lighters, a ferro rod, fatwood sticks, petroleum-jelly cotton balls in a pill bottle, the hatchet.
