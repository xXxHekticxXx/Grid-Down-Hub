## When this applies
Ridgelines, tying down tarps and loads in the truck, hanging food, rescue lines.

## Do this
1. **Bowline:** a loop that won't slip or jam. Make a small loop in the standing line ("the rabbit's hole"), pass the end up through it, around behind the standing line, and back down the hole. Pull tight. Use: end of a ridgeline, a rescue loop around a person's chest.
2. **Clove hitch:** two turns around a pole, the second crossing over the first and tucking under itself. Quick to tie and adjust. Back it up with a half hitch.
3. **Taut-line hitch:** for tent and tarp guy lines. Loop the end around the stake, then make two turns inside the loop around the standing line, then one turn outside. Slide to tighten.
4. **Trucker's hitch:** for a tight ridgeline or truck load. Make a fixed loop in the line (a slipped overhand), run the end around the anchor and back through the loop, pull down for leverage, finish with two half hitches.
5. **Square knot:** right over left, then left over right. Joins two ends of the same size (bandages, bundles). Not for critical loads.
6. **Two half hitches:** around a post, tie a half hitch twice around the standing line. The quick, reliable tie-off.

## Warning signs: get help
- For anything holding a person's weight, use proper climbing rope and gear, not paracord.

## Do NOT
- Do not use a square knot to join ropes that will be loaded hard; use a sheet bend.

## What you need
- 100 feet of paracord, a few ratchet straps. Practice each knot until you can tie it in the dark.
