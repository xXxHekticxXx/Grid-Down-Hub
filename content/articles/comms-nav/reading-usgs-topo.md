## When this applies
Using the 27 USGS quads in the Library (and the Topo overlay on the map) to plan routes and find water and high ground.

## Do this
1. **Colors (USGS):** brown contours = elevation; blue = lakes, streams, springs and wetlands; green = woodland; red and black lines = roads, trails, boundaries and other features; gray or red tint = built-up areas.
2. **Contours:** each line joins points of equal elevation; they never cross. **Index contours** are thicker with elevation numbers. Lines close together = steep; far apart = flat.
3. **In Florida:** relief is low (the Lake Wales and Mount Dora ridges are the high ground). Watch for **marsh symbols** (blue tufts) and closed contours with ticks inside (depressions, often sinkhole lakes and wet prairies).
4. **Scale:** the US Topo quads are 1:24,000: one inch on paper = 2,000 feet; the scale bar is at the bottom.
5. **Grid:** US Topo sheets carry UTM/USNG grid lines every 1,000 m; read right, then up. See [Grid coordinates (USNG)](#/a/usng-coordinates).
6. **Date:** these quads are 2015 editions. Roads and buildings may have changed; the vector map (2026 OpenStreetMap) is more current. Use both.
7. **On the app:** Map → Layers → USGS topo turns the overlay on with an opacity slider; the Library opens each full sheet.

## Warning signs: get help
- If the map and the ground disagree, trust the ground and re-orient.

## Do NOT
- Do not plan a route straight across a blue marsh symbol; Florida wetlands can be impassable.

## What you need
- The topo PDFs (Library), printed ones in a waterproof bag in the truck, a compass.
