## When this applies
Phone dead, GPS failing under thick canopy, or you just want a backup. The Ocala NF has hundreds of similar sand roads and few landmarks.

## Do this
1. **Declination:** the compass needle points to magnetic north, which is about **6.9° west of true north around Sorrento in 2026** (World Magnetic Model 2025; Tools → Compass shows the exact value for your spot and date). USGS topo sheets print an older value; use the app's.
2. **Map bearing to compass bearing:** measure the true bearing on the map, then **add the west declination** (true 90° → compass about 97°). Compass to map: subtract it.
3. **Orient the map:** turn the map so map north matches true north (needle pointing about 7° left of map north).
4. **Find yourself:** match features around you (roads, lakes, power lines, the edge of a pine plantation) to the map. Two bearings to known landmarks crossing on the map give your position.
5. **Walk a bearing:** set the bearing, hold the compass level in front of you, pick a landmark on that line, walk to it, repeat.
6. **Pace count:** count every time your left foot hits; about 60 to 70 double-steps per 100 meters on flat ground for most adults (measure yours).
7. **Aim off:** when heading for a point on a road, aim deliberately a little to one side so you know which way to turn when you reach the road.
8. **Handrails:** follow linear features (roads, power lines, lake shores) instead of straight lines through scrub.

## Warning signs: get help
- Truly lost and getting dark: stop, make camp and signal; see [Signaling for help](#/a/signaling-for-help).

## Do NOT
- Do not hold the compass near the truck, a gun, a knife or the phone; metal and magnets throw it off.
- Do not trust the phone's compass without calibrating it (figure-8 motion).

## What you need
- A baseplate compass with a declination adjustment, the topo PDFs (Library) printed or on the phone, a pencil.
- [Reading USGS topo maps](#/a/reading-usgs-topo) and [Grid coordinates (USNG)](#/a/usng-coordinates).
