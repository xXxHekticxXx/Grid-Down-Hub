## When this applies
No compass, no phone. Basic direction is enough to reach a road, a power line or a lake you know.

## Do this
1. **Sun:** rises in the east and sets in the west (northeast and northwest in summer, southeast and southwest in winter). **Around solar noon it's due south** from Central Florida.
2. **Shadow stick (ATP 3-50.21 ch. 7):** push a straight stick upright in level ground. Mark the tip of its shadow. Wait 15 minutes, mark the tip again. A line from the first mark to the second runs roughly west to east. Stand with the first mark on your left: you're facing north.
3. **North Star (Polaris):** find the Big Dipper; the two stars at the end of its bowl point to Polaris, about five times their spacing away. Polaris is due north and only about 29° above the horizon here.
4. **Southern Cross** isn't visible usefully from Florida; use Orion's belt instead: it rises due east and sets due west.
5. **Big features:** in the Ocala NF, SR 19 runs north-south through the middle and SR 40 runs east-west; heading east or west from most of the forest you'll hit SR 19 or the St. Johns River.

## Warning signs: get help
- Getting dark, injured or low on water: stop moving, make shelter, signal.

## Do NOT
- Do not rely on moss or tree growth for direction; it's unreliable.

## What you need
- A stick, the sun or the stars, a mental map of the main roads.
