## When this applies
Talking with neighbors, people at camp, or between vehicles when cell service is down.

## Do this
1. **FRS (no license):** 22 shared channels. **Channels 1 to 7 and 15 to 22: up to 2 W; channels 8 to 14: 0.5 W** (FCC). Expect about ½ mile on the low-power channels and up to about 2 miles on the 2 W channels, more from high ground.
2. **GMRS (license):** same channels plus repeater channels; handhelds up to 5 W, mobiles and base stations up to 50 W on the main channels, with external antennas. Range of several to 20+ miles with a good antenna, more through repeaters. **License:** apply online with the FCC (ULS); no exam; it covers your immediate family for 10 years. The fee is set by the FCC each year (about $35).
3. **Agree on a plan** with neighbors: a primary channel and a backup, privacy codes (CTCSS) if you use them, and check-in times (for example, on the hour for 5 minutes).
4. **Listen before you talk;** keep it short; anyone can hear you.
5. **Carry spare batteries** or AA-capable radios.
6. **Many local groups monitor channel 20 or GMRS 20 (462.675)** for calling and emergencies; confirm what your area uses.

## Warning signs: get help
- An emergency call heard on the radio: get details and location, relay to 911 if you can reach it.

## Do NOT
- Do not transmit on GMRS frequencies with higher power without a license.
- Do not discuss supplies or plans you don't want strangers to hear.

## What you need
- At least two radios per household, spare batteries, a written channel plan in My Plan → Radio.
