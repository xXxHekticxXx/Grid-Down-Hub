## When this applies
When you want reliable communication over tens of miles (or farther) and access to local emergency networks.

## Do this
1. **Listen now (no license needed):** a handheld scanner or dual-band radio tuned to local repeaters and the national 2-meter simplex calling frequency **146.520 MHz** shows you who's active.
2. **Get the Technician license:** a 35-question multiple-choice test (pass with 26) given by volunteer examiners, no Morse code (FCC, ARRL). Study free online practice tests; many people pass in a few weeks. The FCC charges an application fee.
3. **Tech privileges:** all bands above 30 MHz (2 m, 70 cm) for local and repeater contact, and some HF privileges.
4. **Join Lake County ARES or a local club:** they run nets during storms and help new hams.
5. **Program** local repeaters and simplex frequencies into your radio and write them in My Plan → Radio.

## Warning signs: get help
- In a true emergency to save life, anyone may transmit on any frequency to call for help (FCC rules allow it); say "emergency" and your location.

## Do NOT
- Do not transmit on ham bands without a license except in a genuine life-or-death emergency.
- Do not buy a radio without learning how to program it; practice before you need it.

## What you need
- A dual-band handheld, a better antenna (the stock one is weak), spare batteries, and the Technician study guide.
