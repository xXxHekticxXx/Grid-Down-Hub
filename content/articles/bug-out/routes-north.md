## When this applies
Driving from Sorrento into the Ocala National Forest or north out of Central Florida. The map shows every road (OpenStreetMap, October 2026); the Routes layer draws these as lines.

## Do this
1. **Primary (roughly an hour to Alexander Springs):** **CR 437 north** from SR 46 at Sorrento to **SR 44**, **west on SR 44** into Eustis, then **north on SR 19** through Umatilla and Altoona into the forest. **Alexander Springs:** right on **CR 445** off SR 19. **Juniper Springs:** SR 19 north to **SR 40**, then west. **Salt Springs:** SR 19 north past SR 40.
2. **Backup 1 (back roads, skips Eustis):** CR 437 north to SR 44, **CR 439 north** to **CR 42**, then **west on CR 42** to SR 19 at Altoona.
3. **Backup 2 (east side):** SR 44 **northeast** toward Cassia and Paisley, then **CR 42 west** along the south edge of the forest.
4. **Backup 3 (west, toward Ocala):** **US 441** north through Mount Dora and Leesburg toward Ocala, then **SR 40 east** into the forest.
5. **Avoid** I-4, the Turnpike and Orlando during an evacuation: they jam first.
6. **Inside the forest:** forest roads (FR numbers) are sand, some deep and soft. With a two-wheel-drive truck, stay on paved and graded roads; airing down helps in soft sand.
7. **Fuel:** know your range (Tools → Fuel range) and where stations are (Map → Gas layer); assume many won't pump without power.
8. **Before you go:** look over the route on the map in daylight and pin your destination and two fallback camps.
9. **Drive in daylight,** with the radio on for road closures (CB 19, NOAA radio, local AM/FM).

## Warning signs: get help
- Road closed by water or trees: turn around; don't push through.
- Traffic stopped for hours: switch to a backup early.

## Do NOT
- Do not rely on one route.
- Do not drive forest sand roads after dark without knowing them.

## What you need
- The map packs, printed topo sheets for the forest, a full tank and cans.
