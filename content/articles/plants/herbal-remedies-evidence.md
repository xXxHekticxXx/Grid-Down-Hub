## When this applies
Pharmacies are closed, and you want to know which plant remedies are worth trying for minor problems. This page gives the honest label for each. Remedies are for comfort with minor problems; they don't treat infections, bleeding or emergencies.

## Do this
- **Ginger (some evidence):** tea from fresh or dried root may help nausea; research supports it for pregnancy nausea, less for motion sickness (NCCIH). Side effects: heartburn, stomach upset.
- **Aloe gel on the skin (some evidence):** may speed burn healing. See [Aloe vera](#/a/aloe-vera).
- **Honey on burns (some clinical evidence):** see [Honey for wounds and burns](#/a/honey-wounds). Also soothes coughs in adults and children over 1.
- **Elderberry, cooked (limited evidence):** may relieve cold and flu symptoms (preliminary research, NCCIH). Raw berries, leaves and stems are toxic.
- **Chamomile tea (limited):** likely safe as tea; not enough reliable evidence for sleep or stomach (NCCIH). Avoid if allergic to ragweed.
- **Garlic (limited):** small effects on cholesterol and blood pressure in supplements; no good evidence for colds (NCCIH). Raw garlic on the skin causes chemical burns.
- **Willow bark (traditional, related to aspirin):** contains salicin, which the body converts to a salicylate. Bitter tea from inner bark for aches. Same cautions as aspirin: not for children or teens, not with blood thinners or aspirin allergy.
- **Plantain leaf (Plantago, traditional):** chewed or crushed leaf poultice on stings and itchy bites; not well studied.
- **Yarrow (traditional):** used to slow bleeding from small cuts; not proven. Never in place of pressure.

## Warning signs: get help
- Any symptom that is getting worse, fever over 3 days, or signs listed in Trauma & First Aid pages.

## Do NOT
- Do not stop prescribed medicine for an herb.
- Do not use herbs during pregnancy without medical advice.
- Do not combine herbs with blood thinners, diabetes or seizure medicines without asking a pharmacist; interactions happen (NCCIH).

## What you need
- Dried ginger and chamomile tea bags keep well in the pantry; honey; a potted aloe.
