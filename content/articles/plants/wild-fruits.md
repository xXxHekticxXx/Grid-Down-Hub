## When this applies
Fruit is the easiest wild food to identify correctly. Most of these grow along fences, field edges and clearings around Sorrento.

## Do this
1. **Blackberries and dewberries** (April to June): thorny canes, leaves in 3s or 5s, clustered berries that turn black. Pick fully ripe.
2. **Wild blueberries** (spring to early summer): small shrubs in pine flatwoods and scrub; blue berries with a five-pointed crown on the end.
3. **Chickasaw and flatwoods plums** (late spring): small red, yellow or purple plums, tart to sweet (UF/IFAS).
4. **Muscadine grapes** (August to October): thick-skinned dark purple or bronze grapes on vines with round, toothed, heart-shaped leaves. Eat the pulp; seeds and skin are edible but tough.
5. **Maypop / passionflower** (summer and fall): a spectacular purple fringed flower; the egg-sized green fruit turns yellowish and wrinkly when ripe. Eat the pulp around the seeds.
6. **Elderberry** (summer): flat-topped clusters of tiny dark purple berries. **Eat only cooked ripe berries.** Raw or unripe berries, leaves and stems contain cyanide-producing compounds that cause vomiting and severe diarrhea; cooking removes it (NCCIH).

## Warning signs: get help
- Vomiting, cramps or diarrhea after eating wild fruit: stop, hydrate ([Diarrhea and oral rehydration](#/a/diarrhea-ors)) and call Poison Help if severe.

## Do NOT
- Do not eat grape-like berries on a vine unless you see real grape leaves and tendrils: Virginia creeper (5 leaflets) and moonseed (one seed, crescent-shaped) are poisonous look-alikes.
- Do not eat lantana berries, nightshade berries, pokeweed berries or Brazilian pepper berries.
- Do not eat raw elderberries.

## What you need
- A bag or bucket, long sleeves, a pot for elderberries.
