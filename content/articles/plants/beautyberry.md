## When this applies
Common in Central Florida woods and edges. Late summer to winter, the clusters of magenta-purple berries ringing the stems are unmistakable.

## Do this
1. **Identify:** a shrub 3 to 8 ft, opposite soft leaves with toothed edges, tight clusters of small purple berries at each leaf pair around the stem.
2. **Berries:** edible but bland and a little astringent; traditionally made into jelly with lots of sugar (UF/IFAS). Eat a small handful at most raw.
3. **Bug repellent:** crush a handful of fresh leaves and rub them on exposed skin and clothing. USDA researchers isolated compounds (callicarpenal, intermedeol) from the leaves that repelled mosquitoes and ticks in lab tests. **Evidence: lab studies only;** it isn't an EPA-registered repellent.

## Warning signs: get help
- Skin rash from the leaves: wash off and stop.

## Do NOT
- Do not rely on beautyberry instead of DEET or picaridin where mosquito-borne disease is a risk (CDC recommends EPA-registered repellents).

## What you need
- Your hands. Reapply leaves every hour or so.
