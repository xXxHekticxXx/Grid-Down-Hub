## When this applies
Prickly pear grows in the sandy scrub of the Ocala NF and dry fields in Lake County.

## Do this
1. **Identify:** flat green paddle-shaped pads with clusters of spines and tiny barbed bristles (glochids), yellow flowers in spring, red-purple fruit later.
2. **Handle with tongs or a folded cloth,** never bare hands.
3. **Remove spines:** burn them off over a fire, or scrape both sides and edges with a knife, then peel.
4. **Pads (nopales):** young pads sliced and boiled or grilled, slightly slimy like okra.
5. **Fruit:** burn or scrub off glochids, cut off ends, slice the skin and peel; eat the flesh (seeds are hard, swallow or spit).

## Warning signs: get help
- Glochids in the skin: lift them with tape or glue, then wash. A doctor if many are in the eyes or mouth.

## Do NOT
- Do not eat any cactus or succulent with milky sap.
- Do not skip removing glochids; they lodge in the mouth and throat.

## What you need
- Tongs, a knife, gloves, a fire.
