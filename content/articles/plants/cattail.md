## When this applies
Cattails line lake edges, ditches and marshes throughout Lake County.

## Do this
1. **Identify:** tall flat strap-like leaves growing from the base, a round stem, and the brown "hot dog" seed head on a stalk. Leaves have a spongy pith inside.
2. **Shoots and lower stem core** (spring): pull a young shoot, peel to the white tender core near the base, eat raw or cooked like cucumber.
3. **Green flower spikes** (before they turn brown): boil and eat like corn on the cob.
4. **Pollen** (yellow, late spring): shake into a bag; mix into flour or pancakes.
5. **Roots (rhizomes):** starchy; crush in water and let starch settle, or roast. Fibrous.
6. **Fluff from old heads** makes excellent tinder; see [Starting a fire](#/a/fire-starting).

## Warning signs: get help
- Any plant without the brown seed head, or with leaves arranged like a flat fan (iris, some poisonous) is not cattail.

## Do NOT
- Do not gather from polluted water: cattails absorb contaminants. Avoid ditches by roads and farm runoff.
- Do not wade into marshes without watching for alligators and cottonmouths.
- Don't confuse with blue flag iris (toxic), which has flat fan-arranged leaves and no cattail head.

## What you need
- A knife, a bag, gloves.
