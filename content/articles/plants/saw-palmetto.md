## When this applies
Saw palmetto dominates the understory of the Ocala National Forest scrub and flatwoods. Its berries ripen black in late summer and fall.

## Do this
1. **Identify:** fan-shaped leaves on stems with small sharp saw teeth along the edges, often growing in dense low clumps with trunks creeping along the ground.
2. **Berries:** ripe ones are black and oily, about ¾ inch. Edible raw but strong and soapy; better dried, or cooked into tea.
3. **Herbal use:** saw palmetto extract is a widely sold supplement for prostate symptoms; NCCIH reviews found it no better than placebo for that use.
4. **Stem heart:** the growing tip can be cut and eaten like swamp cabbage. It's small.

## Warning signs: get help
- Stomach upset is the usual side effect of eating many berries.

## Do NOT
- Do not walk through palmetto in shorts or at night: the stems cut and rattlesnakes rest under them. Watch for diamondbacks.
- Florida requires a state permit (Florida Department of Agriculture) and the landowner's written permission to harvest saw palmetto berries. Don't strip public land.

## What you need
- Gloves, a bag.
