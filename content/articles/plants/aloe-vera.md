## When this applies
Small burns and sunburn after cooling, scrapes. Aloe grows easily in pots and gardens across Central Florida.

## Do this
1. **Cool the burn first** with running water (see [Burns](#/a/burns)).
2. **Cut a lower leaf** at the base, slice it open lengthwise, and scoop out the clear inner gel.
3. **Spread a thin layer** on the cooled burn or sunburn; cover small open burns with a non-stick dressing. AHA/Red Cross 2024 list aloe as a reasonable option for small partial-thickness burns after cooling.
4. **Evidence (NCCIH):** clinical research suggests topical aloe gel may speed burn healing and reduce burn pain. Evidence is moderate for skin use.

## Warning signs: get help
- Big, deep, or face/hand burns need a doctor whatever you put on them.
- Rash or itching from the gel: wash it off and stop.

## Do NOT
- **Do not eat aloe or use the yellow latex under the skin** as a laxative: FDA pulled it from laxatives for lack of safety data, and whole-leaf extract is a possible carcinogen (NCCIH).
- Do not put aloe into deep or dirty wounds.

## What you need
- A potted aloe plant, a knife, a clean container.
