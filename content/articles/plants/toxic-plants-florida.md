## When this applies
Learn these before foraging, and before letting the dog chew things. Several grow wild around Sorrento and in the forest; others are common landscape plants at abandoned homes.

## Do this
Recognize and avoid:
1. **Spotted water hemlock (Cicuta maculata):** one of the most toxic plants in North America. Wet ditches, marshes and lake edges throughout Florida. Tall, branched, hollow stems often streaked purple, compound leaves with toothed leaflets, umbrella-shaped clusters of tiny white flowers. All parts deadly, roots most of all; mistaken for wild carrot or parsnip (UF/IFAS). **Avoid every wetland plant with umbrella-shaped white flower clusters.**
2. **Rosary pea:** a vine with shiny red seeds with a black spot. A chewed seed can kill.
3. **Castor bean:** large star-shaped leaves, often reddish, spiny seed pods. Seeds contain ricin.
4. **Oleander:** common landscape shrub with narrow leathery leaves and pink, white or red flowers. All parts toxic to people and dogs, even smoke from burning it.
5. **Sago palm (king sago), cardboard palm and native coontie:** stiff, palm-like cycads; seeds especially toxic, a common cause of fatal liver failure in dogs (Florida Museum).
6. **Jimsonweed (Datura) and angel's trumpet:** big trumpet flowers and spiny seed pods; cause delirium and death.
7. **Nightshades, pokeweed and lantana:** don't eat the berries.
8. **Poison ivy, poison sumac and poisonwood:** rash; see [Poison ivy rash](#/a/poison-ivy).
9. **Mushrooms:** don't eat wild mushrooms.

## Warning signs: get help
- After eating any unknown plant: vomiting, drooling, seizures, confusion, racing or slow heartbeat, trouble breathing. **911 and Poison Help 1-800-222-1222.** Take the plant or a photo.
- The dog chewed a sago or coontie seed, oleander or rosary pea: emergency vet now.

## Do NOT
- Do not burn oleander or poison ivy.
- Do not handle castor bean or rosary pea seeds with cut hands, or let kids use them as beads.
