## When this applies
Small burns and shallow wounds when you're out of ointment, or as your first choice for minor burns after cooling.

## Do this
1. **Cool burns or clean wounds first** (running water until free of debris).
2. **Spread a thin layer of honey** on a non-stick dressing or directly on the burn, then cover.
3. **Change daily,** clean with water, and reapply.
4. **Evidence:** the 2024 AHA/Red Cross first aid guidelines say petrolatum, petrolatum-based antibiotic ointment, honey or aloe vera may be reasonable for small partial-thickness burns managed at home; studies show improved healing times.
5. Medical-grade honey (sterilized) is best; plain store honey is the field substitute.

## Warning signs: get help
- Infection signs (spreading redness, pus, fever), large burns, deep wounds: see a doctor.

## Do NOT
- **Never give honey to an infant under 1 year** by mouth (botulism risk).
- Do not use honey on deep puncture wounds or bites in place of medical care.

## What you need
- A squeeze bottle of honey in the first aid kit, non-stick dressings.
