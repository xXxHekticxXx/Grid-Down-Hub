## When this applies
Cabbage palms grow across Lake County and the forest edges. Hurricanes knock many down: a downed palm is the right time to harvest the heart.

## Do this
1. **Identify:** a single straight trunk, fan-shaped leaves with a curved midrib (costapalmate), often with old leaf bases ("boots") criss-crossing the trunk.
2. **Use a downed or doomed palm.** Removing the bud kills it (UF/IFAS).
3. **Cut away the outer leaf bases** at the top of the trunk with a machete or hatchet until you reach the tender, white, cylindrical heart.
4. **Eat it raw** (crisp, a little like cabbage) or **cook it:** sliced and boiled or stewed with bacon, traditional "swamp cabbage."
5. **The black berries** (fall) were eaten by Native Floridians; thin flesh, mostly seed.

## Warning signs: get help
- None specific; it's a common traditional food. Bitter pieces near the outer layers can be trimmed off.

## Do NOT
- Do not cut down healthy palms on public land or others' property; harvesting on public land is prohibited.
- Do not confuse it with the sago palm (cycad, stiff feather-like leaves, toxic) or other ornamental palms.

## What you need
- A machete, a hatchet, gloves (leaf bases are sharp).
