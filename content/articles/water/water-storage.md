## When this applies
Before hurricane season (June 1 to November 30), before a forecast storm, and any time you rotate supplies. With the grid down, the well pump stops when the generator or solar runs out, and city water may be unsafe.

## Do this
1. **Count it (Ready.gov):** at least **1 gallon per person per day** for drinking and basic hygiene. A resting adult drinks about ¾ gallon; in hot weather needs can double. Working outside in a Florida summer, plan on 1½ to 2 gallons for yourself.
2. **The dog:** about 1 ounce per pound per day, so about ¾ gallon for 90 lb, more in heat.
3. **Targets:** at least 2 weeks at home (Ready.gov suggests several days minimum; two weeks is the common recommendation for hurricanes), and 3 days in the truck.
4. **Containers:** buy commercially bottled water and keep it sealed, cool and dark. For your own containers use food-grade jugs or barrels (blue HDPE marked for water). Clean with dish soap, then sanitize with **1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per quart of water**, swish, empty, and fill with chlorinated tap or treated water.
5. **Rotate** home-filled water every 6 months; write the fill date on the cap.
6. **Before a storm:** fill the camper's fresh tank, every clean jug, coolers, and a clean bathtub or tote for flushing and washing.
7. **Store off the ground,** out of sun and away from fuel and chemicals (plastic absorbs fumes).

## Warning signs: get help
- Running low with no safe source: switch to finding and treating water early, not when you're out. See [Finding water around Sorrento and the forest](#/a/finding-water).

## Do NOT
- Do not ration drinking water: drink what you need today and look for more tomorrow (Ready.gov). Reduce your need by staying cool and resting in the heat of the day.
- Do not reuse milk or juice jugs for long-term storage; they leak and are hard to clean.
- Do not store water containers where gasoline or pesticides are kept.

## What you need
- Water calculator above.
- Bottled water, 5-gallon food-grade jugs or a 55-gallon barrel with a hand pump, unscented bleach, a marker.
