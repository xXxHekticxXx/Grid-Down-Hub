## When this applies
Stored water is running low, or you're on the move or camping.

## Do this
**At home (safe without treatment if the supply was safe):**
1. **Water heater tank:** 30 to 50 gallons. Turn off its power or gas first. Close the cold supply valve, open a hot tap to let air in, drain from the valve at the bottom. Let sediment settle.
2. **Pipes:** open the highest faucet, then drain the lowest one.
3. **Ice cubes and the camper's fresh tank.**
4. **Toilet tank** (not the bowl), only if no chemical cleaners were used: treat it anyway.

**Outside (always treat):**
- **Rain:** a tarp or the camper roof into clean containers; skip the first rush that washes off dirt and bird droppings. Treat before drinking.
- **Springs, lakes and rivers:** flowing water is generally better than stagnant (EPA). In the Ocala NF, Alexander Springs, Juniper Springs, Silver Glen Springs and Salt Springs are reliable clear sources; the map's Water layer shows springs, lakes and public taps. Every spring and lake also has alligators: fill from a spot with a clear view, not at dusk, and don't let the dog wade.
- **Ponds and ditches** near roads, farms or homes: last choice; filter and then disinfect.
- **Morning dew** on grass can be mopped with a cloth (ATP 3-50.21 chapter 3); slow but works.

## Warning signs: get help
- Floating material, dark color, fuel smell or a chemical sheen: don't use it (EPA).
- Algae blooms (green paint or scum on lakes): toxins aren't removed by boiling or filters.

## Do NOT
- Do not drink floodwater even after treatment if another source exists.
- Do not drink from a water heater that was submerged by flooding.
- Do not drink pool water (chemicals) except as a last resort after filtering; use it for flushing and washing.

## What you need
- A hose and a short drain hose for the water heater, a tarp, 5-gallon buckets, your filter and bleach.
- The Map's Water layer, and in the Library: ATP 3-50.21, [Chapter 3 Water](#/book/atp/atp-ch06).
