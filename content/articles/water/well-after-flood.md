## When this applies
Your property is on a private well and floodwater reached the wellhead, or the water turned cloudy, smelly or off-tasting after a storm.

## Do this
1. **Stop drinking it.** Use bottled, stored or boiled water (rolling boil 1 minute) until the well tests clean.
2. **Turn off power to the pump** before inspecting a flooded well; clear debris from the top.
3. **Have a qualified electrician or well contractor** repair a flooded pump or wiring if needed.
4. **Run the water until it's clear,** using the outside spigot closest to the well, so dirty water stays out of the camper plumbing.
5. **Disinfect the well (shock chlorination)** following CDC's step-by-step for drilled wells, or have a well contractor do it.
6. **Test:** wait **7 to 10 days** after disinfection so the chlorine has flushed, then test for total coliform and E. coli through the Lake County health department or a certified lab. Keep boiling until results are clean.
7. **Retest** in 2 to 4 weeks and again in 3 to 4 months.

## Warning signs: get help
- Coliform bacteria in the test: disinfect again and retest; if still positive, contact the health department.
- Fuel or chemical odor: don't use the water at all; testing for chemicals is different.

## Do NOT
- Do not drink well water that was underwater until it tests clean.
- Do not work on a submerged pump with the power on.

## What you need
- Unscented bleach, the EPA Safe Drinking Water Hotline (800-426-4791) for test lab information, bottled water.
