## When this applies
Adding protein when the pantry runs low, at home or camping in the forest.

## Do this
1. **License:** Florida requires a freshwater fishing license (FWC); some exemptions exist, such as residents fishing on their own homestead in their county and seniors 65+. Buy one now; the FWC Fish|Hunt FL app holds it on your phone.
2. **Panfish (bluegill, shellcracker):** small hooks (#6 to #10), a light sinker, a bobber, worms or crickets, fished near docks, weeds and fallen trees.
3. **Catfish:** a bigger hook (#2 to 2/0), on the bottom, with cut bait, chicken liver or worms, evenings and night.
4. **Bass:** artificial worms or spinners along vegetation edges, early and late in the day.
5. **Clean fish quickly:** keep them alive on a stringer or on ice; gut and scale or fillet; cook to 145 °F.
6. **Limit lines in the water** and check traps and lines often; FWC rules on limits and gear apply.
7. **Alligators** take hooked fish and come to cleaning spots. Clean fish away from the water's edge and dispose of scraps in the trash, not the lake.

## Warning signs: get help
- Fishhook in the skin: push and cut the barb, or go to a clinic if deep, in a joint or near the eye.
- Algae blooms or posted fish consumption advisories: don't eat fish from that water.

## Do NOT
- Do not fish where the water is posted for blue-green algae.
- Do not leave line and hooks on the bank (birds, the dog).

## What you need
- A spinning rod, a small tackle box (hooks #2 to #10, split shot, bobbers, a few soft plastic worms), pliers, a stringer, a fillet knife, a fishing license.
- Library: ATP 3-50.21, [Chapter 4 Food](#/book/atp/atp-ch07), for fish traps and field methods (check FWC rules before using them).
