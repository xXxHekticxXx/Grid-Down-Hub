## When this applies
The power is out or the generator can't run the fridge. Spoiled food is a common cause of sickness after hurricanes.

## Do this
1. **Note the time the power went out** and **keep the doors closed.**
2. **Fridge: safe for about 4 hours** unopened. **Full freezer: about 48 hours; half-full: about 24 hours.**
3. **Before 4 hours are up,** move milk, meat, leftovers and other perishables into coolers packed with ice, block ice or frozen jugs. Keep coolers at **40 °F or below**; use a thermometer and add ice as it melts. 50 lb of dry ice holds an 18-cubic-foot freezer about 2 days (gloves; never in a closed camper or cab).
4. **Eat in this order:** fridge perishables first, then freezer items as they thaw, then shelf-stable food.
5. **When power returns, check temperatures.** Freezer food that still has ice crystals or is at 40 °F or below can be refrozen.
6. **Throw out** perishables that sat above 40 °F for over 2 hours: meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, soft cheese, leftovers, cut fruit, cooked vegetables, deli meats, casseroles, opened juices, creamy dressings. Opened mayonnaise and similar sauces above 50 °F for over 8 hours.

**Before a storm:** freeze jugs of water to fill freezer space (they become drinking water later), put appliance thermometers in fridge and freezer, freeze what you won't need right away, buy block ice.

## Warning signs: get help
- Vomiting or diarrhea after eating: [Diarrhea and oral rehydration](#/a/diarrhea-ors).

## Do NOT
- **Do not taste food to decide if it's safe.** When in doubt, throw it out.
- Do not keep food outside in "cool" weather; it warms and animals get to it.
- Do not open the freezer to check on it every hour.

## What you need
- Appliance thermometers, two good coolers, frozen water jugs, a cooler thermometer.
