## When this applies
Cooking anything that could make you sick if undercooked: store meat, fish you catch, wild hog, deer, rabbit and squirrel.

## Do this
Cook to these internal temperatures (FoodSafety.gov), measured in the thickest part:
- **Chicken, turkey, any poultry, and anything stuffed:** 165 °F
- **Ground meat and sausage (beef, pork):** 160 °F
- **Venison and rabbit, wild or farmed:** 160 °F
- **Beef, pork, lamb, goat steaks, roasts and chops:** 145 °F, then rest 3 minutes
- **Fish:** 145 °F or until the flesh is no longer see-through and flakes with a fork
- **Shrimp and crab:** until pearly white and opaque
- **Egg dishes:** 160 °F; eggs until yolk and white are firm
- **Leftovers and casseroles:** 165 °F

**Wild hog:** cook thoroughly (at least 160 °F) and wear gloves when cleaning it: wild hogs in Florida can carry brucellosis and other diseases.

## Warning signs: get help
- Fever, vomiting or diarrhea after eating game or fish; tell the doctor what you ate.

## Do NOT
- Do not judge doneness by color alone.
- Do not eat raw or rare wild game, freshwater fish or hog.
- Do not leave cooked food out more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90 °F).

## What you need
- An instant-read thermometer; gloves for cleaning game.
