## When this applies
You have legally taken a squirrel, rabbit, wild hog or other game with the .22 or another firearm, and need to make it safe to eat.

## Do this
1. **Legal first:** a Florida hunting license is required for most hunting; seasons and wildlife management area rules apply (the Ocala NF is a WMA). Wild hogs on private land are exempt from a hunting license (FWC).
2. **Gloves on.** Wild animals can carry tularemia (rabbits), brucellosis (hogs) and parasites.
3. **Squirrel or rabbit:** cut a slit across the back, work two fingers in and pull the skin toward the head and the tail. Cut off head, feet and tail. Open the belly carefully without cutting the guts, scoop them out, rinse the cavity with clean water.
4. **Check the liver:** white spots on a rabbit's liver are a sign of disease. Don't eat it.
5. **Cool the meat fast:** into a cooler with ice, or cook right away in Florida heat.
6. **Cook to 160 °F:** stew, braise or roast. Slow-cooking makes tough game tender.
7. **Bury or bag the scraps** away from camp; they draw bears, hogs and gators.

## Warning signs: get help
- Fever, swollen lymph nodes or an ulcer on the hand days after handling a rabbit (tularemia): see a doctor.

## Do NOT
- Do not eat an animal that acted sick or was found dead.
- Do not shoot near roads, campsites or houses, or on Forest Service roads while transporting firearms in the WMA; see [Firearms in the field](#/a/firearms-in-the-field).

## What you need
- Gloves, a sharp small knife, zip bags, a cooler, a thermometer, clean water.
