## When this applies
Floodwater got into the pantry, the camper or a cooler. Floodwater carries sewage, chemicals and bacteria.

## Do this
1. **Throw out** anything that touched floodwater that isn't in a sealed, undamaged metal can or retort pouch: boxed and bagged food, jars with screw lids or snap caps, home-canned food, produce, and anything in the fridge or freezer the water reached.
2. **Commercial cans and pouches that are undamaged:** remove labels, wash with soap and water, then sanitize by soaking 15 minutes in a solution of 1 tablespoon unscented bleach per gallon of water, or boil 2 minutes. Air-dry and relabel with a marker, including the expiration date.
3. **Throw out** dented, bulging, rusted or leaking cans.
4. **Wash and sanitize** cooking pots, utensils and countertops before using them.

## Warning signs: get help
- Anyone sick after eating: [Diarrhea and oral rehydration](#/a/diarrhea-ors).

## Do NOT
- Do not try to wash and keep jars, boxes or bags that floodwater touched.
- Do not use wooden cutting boards or plastic utensils that sat in floodwater.

## What you need
- Bleach, a bucket, a marker, trash bags.
