# Water and Food Content (verified 2026-10-26)

## How much water to store
*Source: [Ready.gov Build a Kit](https://www.ready.gov/kit)*
- **1 gallon per person per day, for several days,** for drinking and sanitation. Store extra for the dog and for Florida heat. (That last part is Frank's note, not Ready.gov's.)

## Make water safe: boiling (best method)
*Source: [CDC](https://cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/)*
1. If the water is cloudy, filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter, or let it settle and draw off the clear water.
2. Bring it to a **rolling boil for 1 minute.** (Above 6,500 ft, boil 3 minutes. Florida doesn't need this.)
3. Let it cool, then store it in clean, covered containers.
4. To improve the flat taste, pour it back and forth between containers, or add a pinch of salt per quart.

Boiling is the most effective method. Chemical disinfectants don't reliably kill the parasites *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia* ([CDC](https://cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/)). Neither boiling nor bleach removes chemicals, metals or salt ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water)).

## Make water safe: unscented household bleach
*Source: [CDC](https://cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/)*

Use only **regular, unscented** bleach. No scented, color-safe or "with cleaner" bleach. Check the label for sodium hypochlorite percent. Bleach older than about a year loses strength ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water)).

1. Filter or settle cloudy water first.
2. Add bleach using the table below. **If the water is cloudy, murky, colored or very cold, use double.**
3. Stir well.
4. **Wait at least 30 minutes** before drinking.
5. Store it in clean, covered containers.

**Bleach with 5%–9% sodium hypochlorite (most US bleach)**

| Water | Drops | mL | Measuring spoon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 quart / liter | 2 drops | 0.1 mL | too small to measure |
| 1 gallon | 8 drops | 0.5 mL | a little less than 1/8 tsp |
| 5 gallons | 40 drops | 2.5 mL | 1/2 tsp |

**Bleach with 1% sodium hypochlorite**

| Water | Drops | mL | Measuring spoon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 quart / liter | 10 drops | 0.5 mL | 1/8 tsp |
| 1 gallon | 40 drops | 2.5 mL | 1/2 tsp |
| 5 gallons | 200 drops | 12.5 mL | 2 1/2 tsp |

**Calculator logic for the app:** drops = gallons × 8 (5–9% bleach) or gallons × 40 (1% bleach), × 2 if cloudy. Always show the 30-minute wait.

**Use treated water for** drinking, cooking, drinks, washing dishes and brushing teeth ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water)).

## Food safety when the power is out
*Sources: [FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/food-safety-in-disaster-or-emergency) · [USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/emergencies/keep-your-food-safe-during-emergencies)*

**During the outage**
- **Keep fridge and freezer doors shut.**
- The fridge keeps food safe for about **4 hours** unopened.
- A **full freezer** holds temperature about **48 hours** (a half-full one about 24 hours) if unopened.
- For long outages, move food into a cooler with ice and keep it at **40°F or below**. 50 lb of dry ice holds an 18-cubic-foot full freezer for about 2 days. **Never touch dry ice with bare hands.**

**What to throw out**
- All perishable food from the fridge (meat, poultry, fish, eggs, leftovers) after **4 hours** without power.
- Any perishable food above **40°F for 2 hours or more,** or **1 hour if it's above 90°F.** That's common in a Florida summer.
- Thawed perishable food from the freezer.

**What's OK**
- Freezer food that **still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below** can be refrozen or cooked.
- **Never taste food to test it. When in doubt, throw it out.**

**Pantry for outages** ([Ready.gov Food](https://www.ready.gov/food)): canned meats, fruits and vegetables (plus a manual can opener); protein or fruit bars; dry cereal or granola; peanut butter; dried fruit; canned juice; shelf-stable milk; high-energy foods. Choose foods you will actually eat, and include dog food.
