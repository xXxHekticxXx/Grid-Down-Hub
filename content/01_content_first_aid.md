## CPR (adult who suddenly collapses)
*Source: American Heart Association. [Hands-Only CPR](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resources/what-is-cpr) · [2025 Adult BLS Guidelines](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support)*

**Hands-Only CPR (bystanders): two steps**
1. **Call 911** (or send someone to call).
2. **Push hard and fast in the center of the chest.**

**How to do it well**
- Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone) and the other hand on top.
- Push **at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep**, but not more than 2.4 inches (6 cm).
- Push at **100–120 compressions per minute**. *(App: metronome at 110 bpm.)*
- Let the chest come all the way back up between pushes. Don't lean on it.
- Keep interruptions as short as possible. If another person is there, switch every 2 minutes, because compressions get weaker after about 2 minutes.
- Do CPR on a firm, flat surface with the person on their back when you can.
- Trained rescuers may give breaths at **30 compressions : 2 breaths**.
- If an AED is available, turn it on and follow its voice prompts. Restart compressions right after any shock.

## Choking
*Source: [AHA 2025 Guidelines news release](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/updated-cpr-guidelines-tackle-choking-response-opioid-related-emergencies-and-a-revised-chain-of-survival). This guidance changed in October 2025.*

**Conscious adult or child who can't breathe, cough or speak:**
1. Give **5 back blows**.
2. Give **5 abdominal thrusts**.
3. Keep alternating 5 and 5 until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive.
4. If they become unresponsive: call 911 and start CPR.

**Infant (under 1 year):**
1. Give **5 back blows**.
2. Give **5 chest thrusts using the heel of one hand**.
3. Keep alternating until the object comes out or the infant becomes unresponsive.
4. **Do not give abdominal thrusts to infants** (risk of injury).

## Severe bleeding
*Sources: [Stop the Bleed booklet](https://www.in.gov/dhs/files/stop-the-bleed-booklet.pdf) · [American Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/learn-fa-bleeding-life-threatening-external.html)*

**The ABCs of bleeding**
- **A: Alert.** Call 911. Make sure you are safe first.
- **B: Bleeding.** Find the bleeding injury.
- **C: Compress.** Apply pressure to stop the bleeding, using one of the three methods below.

**1. Direct pressure (no kit needed)**
1. Cover the wound with any clean cloth (a shirt works).
2. If the wound is large and deep, stuff the cloth down into it.
3. Push directly on it with both hands, as hard as you can.
4. Keep pressing until the bleeding stops or help takes over.
5. If a bandaged wound starts bleeding again, don't pile on more dressings. Take off the bandage, leave the one dressing on the wound, and press directly on it again (Red Cross).

**2. Tourniquet (life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg)**
1. Wrap the tourniquet around the limb **2–3 inches above the wound**, not on a joint. Go above the joint if needed.
2. Pull the free end as tight as possible and secure it.
3. **Twist the windlass until the bleeding stops.**
4. Lock the windlass in place.
5. **Write down the time** you applied it. It will hurt; that is expected.

**3. Wound packing (neck, shoulder or groin, or a limb with no tourniquet)**
1. Open clothing over the wound and wipe away pooled blood.
2. Pack (stuff) the wound with bleeding-control gauze (best), plain gauze or a clean cloth.
3. Press with both hands, as hard as you can, and keep pressing.

**Object stuck in the wound:** do **not** pull it out. Pack dressings around it and press around it, not on it.

**After bleeding is controlled:** keep the person warm, watch their breathing and alertness, and stay with them.

## Burns
*Sources: [American Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/burns) · [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/)*

1. Remove clothing and jewelry near the burn, **unless it is stuck to the skin**.
2. **Cool the burn under clean, cool running water** (water you could drink). Red Cross says 5–20 minutes; UK guidance says 20 minutes. If there's no running water, use a cool, wet compress.
3. Cover it loosely with plastic wrap or a clean, non-stick cover.

**Do not:** use ice, butter, oils, creams or toothpaste, or pop blisters.
**Get emergency help for:** large or deep burns; burns on the face, genitals or buttocks; chemical or electrical burns; any burn on a baby or child.
On large burns, watch for chilling (hypothermia), especially in children.

## Heat stroke and heat exhaustion
*Source: [CDC Heat-Related Illnesses](https://www.cdc.gov/extreme-heat/media/pdf/Heat_Related_Illness.pdf) · [CDC/NIOSH](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html)*

**Heat stroke: a medical emergency**
- Signs: body temperature 103°F or higher; hot, red, dry or damp skin; fast, strong pulse; headache, dizziness, nausea; **confusion**; passing out.
- What to do: **call 911**, move them somewhere cooler, and cool them fast with cold water, wet cloths, or ice on the neck, armpits and groin while fanning. **Do not give them anything to drink.**

**Heat exhaustion**
- Signs: heavy sweating; cold, pale, clammy skin; fast, weak pulse; nausea or vomiting; muscle cramps; tiredness; dizziness; headache; fainting.
- What to do: move somewhere cool, loosen clothes, apply cool wet cloths or take a cool bath, and sip water.
- **Get medical help right away if** they are vomiting, symptoms get worse, or symptoms last over 1 hour.

**Heat cramps:** stop activity, move somewhere cool, drink water or a sports drink. Get help if cramps last over 1 hour.

## Snakebite (Florida)
*Sources: [Florida Poison Control Centers](https://floridapoisoncontrol.org/poisoning-in-florida/snakes/) · [UF/IFAS](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/uw261)*

**Do:**
1. Move away from the snake. **Don't try to catch or kill it.** If it's safe, take a photo.
2. **Call 911 / get to a hospital right away.** Don't wait for symptoms. Coral snake bites may barely hurt at first.
3. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing near the bite. Swelling comes fast.
4. Keep the bitten limb **below heart level**, keep the person calm, and gently clean the bite.
5. Call **Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222**. They can identify the snake from a photo and coordinate antivenom.

**Do NOT:** use a tourniquet, ice, heat or electric shock; cut the bite; suck out venom; or give alcohol or stimulants.

## Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning
*Source: [CDC Power Outage Safety](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/response/what-to-do-protect-yourself-during-a-power-outage.html)*

- **Symptoms:** headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, chest pain, confusion. People who are asleep or have been drinking can die before feeling anything.
- **What to do:** get everyone, including pets, into fresh air immediately. Call 911 or Poison Control at **1-800-222-1222**.
- Prevention is in `03` (generators, grills, camp stoves). **This matters a lot in a camper,** which CDC names specifically.
